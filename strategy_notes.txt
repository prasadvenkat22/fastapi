================================================================================
QQQ 0DTE TRADING ENGINE — STRATEGY NOTES
================================================================================
Last updated: 2026-08-17
Code: trading_engine/   Config: .env.production.example (all TRADING_* vars)

Everything here is MOCKED. The broker places no real orders. Read the
"Known limits" section before treating any P&L number as meaningful.


================================================================================
1. WHAT THE ENGINE TRADES
================================================================================

Defined-risk debit vertical spreads on QQQ, expiring same day (0DTE).
Long the in-the-money leg, short the at-the-money leg, $3 wide.

  Bullish -> BULL_CALL_SPREAD   long (ATM-3) call, short ATM call
  Bearish -> BEAR_PUT_SPREAD    long (ATM+3) put,  short ATM put

Both directions exist. It is not a call-only strategy.

Why a spread and not a naked long option: max loss is capped at the net debit
paid. A naked long can lose everything with no floor and needs a larger move
to overcome its premium.

Why long ITM / short ATM specifically:
  - The long ITM leg carries little extrinsic value, so there is little time
    premium to lose.
  - The short ATM leg carries the MOST extrinsic value of any strike, and you
    are short it, so its decay works in your favour.
  - Net result is positive theta while price holds above the short strike:
    the position gains value simply by time passing. That is the entire
    reason to buy ITM rather than ATM or OTM.

Max value of the spread is its width ($3.00). Max loss is the debit paid.


================================================================================
2. THE TRADING DAY
================================================================================

  09:30           Bell. Scheduler starts ticking (5 min cycles).
  09:30 - 09:45   WARMUP. No new entries.
                  Opening-auction whipsaws are not a trend, but MACD/EMA/
                  Bollinger/RSI will all read a direction from them and
                  nothing downstream can tell that apart from a real signal.
                  Position MANAGEMENT still runs — stops and take-profits
                  fire from the first cycle. A stop that ignores the first
                  15 minutes is worse than no stop.
  09:45 - 14:00   ENTRY WINDOW. New positions may open here.
  14:00           Entry cutoff. No new positions, no scale-ins.
                  A 0DTE spread opened later has too little day left to work.
  15:45           FORCE CLOSE. Everything flattened regardless of P&L.
                  These expire at today's close: the short leg carries
                  assignment/pin risk and an OTM long leg expires worthless.
  16:00           Expiration.

Config: TRADING_WARMUP_MINUTES (15)


================================================================================
3. HOW AN ENTRY IS DECIDED
================================================================================

Five agents run in parallel each cycle, then a deterministic rule engine
decides. Four are technical, one is macro.

  MACD        12/26/9 histogram   BULLISH >0.01 / BEARISH <-0.01 / NEUTRAL
  Trend       EMA 20 and EMA 50   ABOVE_SMA if price clears both
                                  (field name is legacy — it IS an EMA)
  Bollinger   20-period, 2 sigma  UPPER_BAND / LOWER_BAND / NORMAL
  RSI         Wilder 14           OVERBOUGHT >=70 / OVERSOLD <=30 / NEUTRAL
  Macro       breadth + VIX + Claude on headlines -> GOOD or BAD

An entry needs ALL FOUR technicals in one specific arrangement, PLUS macro
GOOD. Four such arrangements exist:

  BULL FADE          MACD BULLISH, ABOVE, LOWER_BAND, OVERSOLD
                     Stretched down but momentum turning up — drop exhausted.
  BULL CONTINUATION  MACD BULLISH, ABOVE, UPPER_BAND, OVERBOUGHT
                     Everything agrees up — breakout in progress.
  BEAR FADE          MACD BEARISH, BELOW, UPPER_BAND, OVERBOUGHT
                     Stretched up but momentum turning down — rally exhausted.
  BEAR CONTINUATION  MACD BEARISH, BELOW, LOWER_BAND, OVERSOLD
                     Everything agrees down — breakdown in progress.

CONSEQUENCE: entries are RARE. Five conditions must align. Most sessions
produce no entry at all. This is by design, but it means a morning of
watching usually shows nothing but HOLD, and testing changes to the exit
logic requires seeding a position manually.


================================================================================
4. THE MACRO GATE
================================================================================

Sentiment is GOOD only if ALL of these hold. Any one flips it to BAD.

  Breadth positive       addq > 0                    (level)
  Breadth not collapsing >= -0.40 vs 30-min peak     (VELOCITY)
  VIX level              < 22.0                      (level)
  VIX session move       < +10% from 09:30 open      (VELOCITY)
  Claude verdict         GOOD

The two velocity terms exist because level checks alone cannot tell a market
that IS bad from one that is BECOMING bad. VIX at 18 but +30% on the day,
with breadth bled from +45 to +3, reads as perfectly calm on levels alone.

Breadth velocity uses a ROLLING 30-minute peak, not the session peak. A
session-peak anchor reads an ordinary all-day drift as an emergency and then
stays latched for the rest of the day. Verified: a +48 -> +20 bleed over five
hours never trips; the same drop compressed into 15 minutes trips five times.

Config: TRADING_VIX_LEVEL_MAX, TRADING_VIX_SPIKE_PCT,
        TRADING_BREADTH_COLLAPSE_RATIO, TRADING_BREADTH_WINDOW_MINUTES


================================================================================
5. HOW A POSITION EXITS
================================================================================

Five rules, strict priority order. First match wins. The ORDER is the design.

  1. FORCE CLOSE   after 15:45          Overrides P&L entirely.
  2. TAKE PROFIT   >= +30%              Close.
  3. SCALE IN      <= -10%, macro GOOD  Double the position, if affordable,
                                        before 14:00, max 3 times.
  4. STOP LOSS     <= -10%              Scale-in conditions not met — take it.
  5. RISK-OFF CUT  <= -5% AND macro BAD Cut at half the normal stop.

Rule 5 sits LAST despite having the widest threshold. Checking it first would
swallow every losing exit and label them all RISK_OFF. Below rule 4 it owns
only the band between -5% and -10%, so a position that genuinely breached the
full stop is still recorded as STOP_LOSS. Those labels feed the trade-setup
vector store, so keeping them distinct is what makes the record worth
learning from later.

WHY 30% AND NOT 50%:
A debit spread can never be worth more than its width. Max possible gain is
(width - entry debit) / entry debit, and the entry debit RISES through the
day as time value drains — which lowers the ceiling as the session runs on.
Measured on a $3-wide spread:

    entered 09:30   debit 1.85   max gain 61.8%
    entered 11:00   debit 1.90   max gain 57.8%
    entered 13:00   debit 2.01   max gain 49.1%
    entered 14:00   debit 2.12   max gain 41.7%

A 50% target is structurally impossible for anything entered after midday;
40% is impossible after ~13:00. Those positions would simply ride to the
force-close. 30% is reachable at any entry time.

Config: TRADING_TAKE_PROFIT_PCT (30), TRADING_STOP_LOSS_PCT (-10),
        TRADING_RISK_OFF_STOP_LOSS_PCT (-5)


================================================================================
6. CAPITAL AND SIZING
================================================================================

TRADING_POSITION_BUDGET is TOTAL CAPITAL, not per-trade. Only one position is
open at a time, and the worst case on a force-closed loser is the entire
amount deployed.

TRADING_ENTRY_FRACTION (0.4) is the share the opening trade may use. The rest
is held back to fund scale-ins. This MUST stay below 1.0 — with the full
budget deployed there is no cash left, and the scale-in rule can never fire
at any budget.

Because each scale-in DOUBLES the position, how many actually fit is
capital-bound, not governed by TRADING_MAX_SCALE_INS:

    fraction 0.40  ->  1 scale-in
    fraction 0.25  ->  2 scale-ins
    fraction 0.125 ->  3 scale-ins

FLOOR: a spread costs roughly $200. If budget x fraction is below that, the
engine buys zero contracts and silently never trades. No error is raised.

NOTE: doubling is a martingale. Three scale-ins would take 5 contracts to 40,
on a 0DTE position already down 10% with hours to expiry. Exposure is now
capped by capital, but the shape of the strategy is unchanged — consider
fixed-size adds instead if that risk profile is not intended.


================================================================================
7. WHY CREDIT SPREADS MIGHT MAKE SENSE (NOT IMPLEMENTED)
================================================================================

The engine only sells premium indirectly, via the short ATM leg of a debit
spread. It has no credit spread capability at all. Whether to add one:

THE CASE FOR
  - Three ways to win instead of one. A debit spread needs the market to
    move your way. An OTM credit spread profits if the market moves your way,
    stays completely flat, OR moves moderately against you — anything that
    finishes below the short strike.
  - Theta works unconditionally. A debit spread only has positive theta when
    it is ITM. An OTM credit spread decays in your favour always, and on 0DTE
    that decay is enormous.
  - IT FILLS A REAL GAP. The engine currently has NO expression for a flat
    market. Its entry conditions require MACD/Bollinger/RSI alignment, which
    consolidation does not produce, so a quiet tape simply produces HOLD all
    day. Credit spreads are how a flat market becomes tradeable.
  - Selling inflated premium. A brief afternoon short-covering spike inflates
    OTM call premium; selling it right before afternoon decay is a genuinely
    good entry.

THE CASE AGAINST
  - The payoff ratio is poor. A $1-wide spread sold for $0.25 risks $0.75 to
    make $0.25. You need better than a 75% win rate just to break even, and a
    single max loss erases three wins.
  - Gamma risk near expiry is severe. A small adverse move late in the day
    can flip a comfortable winner into a maximum loser very quickly. This is
    the same hyper-gamma risk that justifies the existing 14:00 cutoff, but
    it hurts a short position far more than a long one.
  - It is NOT a small code change. Credit received rather than debit paid,
    P&L inverted (you profit as the spread's value FALLS), max loss larger
    than the credit collected, sizing against margin/buying power rather than
    premium, and every exit rule reversed. MockSpreadPosition.return_pct as
    written produces nonsense for a credit spread.

WHEN THEY MAKE MOST SENSE
  - Flat or slowly bleeding tape, midday (11:00-13:30), when theta
    accelerates and direction is genuinely unclear.
  - Elevated IV — you are selling something expensive.
  - A clear technical level (VWAP, 20 SMA, prior day high) sitting above the
    short strike to insulate it.

VERDICT
Worth adding, but as its own piece of work with its own testing, not bolted
onto the debit path. The honest sequencing is: get the debit strategy
producing real trades and real recorded outcomes first, so there is something
to compare a credit strategy against.

Also note: OTM DEBIT spreads should never be used. They need a large
directional move just to reach breakeven, and on 0DTE you can be completely
right about direction and still lose 100%.


================================================================================
8. KNOWN LIMITS — READ BEFORE TRUSTING ANY NUMBER
================================================================================

  - THE BROKER IS MOCKED. No orders are placed anywhere. Every fill is
    simulated.
  - NO OPTION CHAIN FEED. Spread values come from a model
    (broker.estimate_spread_value): width scaled by a normal CDF centred on
    the midpoint between the strikes, with its spread shrinking as expiry
    nears. It converges to the true expiry payoff and reproduces the right
    theta behaviour, but it is a model, not a quote. TRADING_DAILY_VOL_PCT
    (1.0) is its one free parameter and the main knob on how fast P&L moves.
  - NO SLIPPAGE, NO COMMISSIONS, NO BID/ASK SPREAD. Real fills are worse.
  - $TICKQ IS NOT USED. Tradier does not carry it and there is no honest free
    approximation. The Institutional Divergence Filter that depended on it is
    dropped.
  - BREADTH IS A PROXY. Self-computed from a static 63-stock Nasdaq-100
    basket, not the real index membership, which changes over time.
  - TRADE SETUP VECTORS ARE LOGGING ONLY. query_similar_setups() works but
    nothing consumes it — there are not enough closed trades to learn from.
  - NO TIME-OF-DAY STRUCTURE SELECTION. The same long-ITM/short-ATM shape is
    used at every hour. A momentum-window ATM structure is not implemented.


================================================================================
9. OPERATING IT
================================================================================

  GET  /trading/status               everything at once
  POST /trading/scheduler/start      begin the loop (interval_minutes 1-60)
  POST /trading/scheduler/stop       halt it
  POST /trading/run-daily-cycle      run exactly one cycle by hand
  GET  /trading/position             open spread, repriced
  GET  /trading/history              closed trades and realized P&L
  POST /trading/kill-switch/toggle   ACTIVATE / DEACTIVATE

The scheduler does NOT auto-start. After a deploy it sits idle until
scheduler/start is called — a restart must never silently resume trading.

The kill switch is a KILL_SWITCH.txt file, checked by both the rule engine
and the scheduler before every tick.

TESTING TIP: because entries are rare, the fastest way to exercise the exit
logic is to insert an OpenPosition row priced at roughly -6% and run one
cycle. If macro is GOOD and you need it BAD, set TRADING_VIX_SPIKE_PCT=0
temporarily to force risk-off.
