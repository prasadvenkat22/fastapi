Both numbers are computed before entry, from two inputs the screener has in hand at that moment: the live option quotes and three years of the underlying's daily closes. Nothing else.

How the probability is built

1. Take every day in the last three years and measure the move from that day to the same number of sessions ahead as the candidate's expiry. For a Friday expiry screened on Monday that is four sessions, so about 750 historical four-session moves.
2. Remove the average drift, so the distribution says how far the name moves, not which way it has trended. This is deliberate: a name that rose 40% last year would otherwise make every call spread look good.
3. Apply each of those moves to today's price. That gives about 750 possible prices at expiry.
4. Price the spread at the fill you would actually get: buy the long leg at the ask, sell the short leg at the bid. That is the cost.
5. For each of the 750 prices, compute what the spread is worth at expiry minus that cost.

Pwin is the share of those 750 outcomes where the spread makes any profit at all. Not the maximum, any. A 45% Pwin means that in 45% of the name's own historical four-session moves, applied to today's price, this spread finishes above what you paid.

Need is the win rate the price demands. It is cost divided by width. Pay 4.37 for a spread that can be worth at most 17.50 and you need to win 25% of the time just to break even, because each wi each loss.

What Pwin is not. It is not a directional forecast. It only says how often a move of this size has happened. Direction is left to the macro veto, the news tilt and the tape gates.
