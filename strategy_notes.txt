================================================================================
QQQ 0DTE TRADING ENGINE - STRATEGY NOTES
================================================================================

Current as of 2026-08-22. Every number here is read from the deployed
configuration, not from intent.

This file describes WHAT the engine does. The WHY lives beside each parameter
in trading_engine/playbook.py and trading_engine/nodes.py, where the sweep
that chose it is quoted in full. Nothing here is a preference: every value was
measured with scripts/sweep.py over 60 sessions, and several were measured
more than once because the tool producing the evidence turned out to be wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE ENGINE TRADES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

QQQ options expiring the same day. Vertical spreads only - never a naked leg,
never a single long option. One position at a time, held in a single
trading_open_positions row.

Two structures:

  DEBIT VERTICAL (morning). Buy the nearer strike, sell the further one. Pays
  if QQQ moves the right way; loses the premium paid if it does not.

  CREDIT VERTICAL (afternoon). Sell the nearer strike, buy the further one for
  protection. Pays if the short strike holds, whichever way price wanders
  below it. Risks width-minus-credit.

Two windows are live. Two more are defined and switched off - they stay in the
code so a position opened under one can still be priced and exited if it is
ever re-enabled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TRADING DAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  09:30  Market opens. No entries for 15 minutes - the opening auction
         produces whipsaws the indicators read as trend.
  09:45  Warmup ends. Entries become possible in BOTH directions - the
         warmup gates the whole entry branch, not the short side only.
         This is the earliest minute any entry can fire; section 27
         swept opening MORNING_DRIFT here and it is the worst of five.
  10:15  MORNING_DRIFT opens.
  11:30  MORNING_DRIFT closes to new entries. A position already open keeps
         running.
  13:25  Handoff. Any morning position is closed so the slot is free.
  13:30  AFTERNOON_CREDIT opens.
  14:00  Debit cutoff - no new DEBIT entries after this hour. Credit windows
         are exempt: a credit spread wants less time left, not more.
  15:00  AFTERNOON_CREDIT closes to new entries.
  15:45  Force close. Everything is flattened regardless of P&L. Expiry is
         16:15; this leaves half an hour of contract life so the exit is
         priced with real time value rather than marked to intrinsic.

A cron job runs one cycle a minute (scripts/run_cycle.py). It owns the
market-hours check itself, in America/New_York, because a UTC crontab window
would be wrong for half the year.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. INDICATORS - EXACTLY WHAT IS COMPUTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All are computed over 5-minute QQQ bars, with five days of history so the
longer averages are warmed up. (A backtest that used only the current
session's bars produced materially different results - see section 11.)

  MACD          12/26/9 EMAs. Histogram > 0.01 is BULLISH, < -0.01 BEARISH,
                otherwise NEUTRAL.

  Trend         EMA(20) and EMA(50), not simple averages. ABOVE_SMA requires
  (sma_trend)   the close above BOTH. EMA rather than SMA so this filter reacts
                on the same timescale as MACD - a laggy SMA confirms a trend
                after most of a 0DTE day's move is gone.

  9 EMA         Trailing reference only, never an entry filter. ABOVE_EMA9 /
                BELOW_EMA9.

  EMA cross     EMA(9) against the SIMPLE 20 average - velocity rather than
                position. Simple, not exponential, and measured: swept on
                2026-08-29 the simple 20 gave +79.34 a day against +78.75
                for EMA(21) and +76.41 for EMA(20), with a far better
                second half (-6.86 against -79) and a $91 better worst
                day. This file previously described it as EMA(20), which
                the code never did. See section 35.

  50 EMA        The bar's HIGH pierces the 50 EMA but the CLOSE finishes below
  rejection     it. Recorded; used only by the REJECT tier.

  VWAP          Session VWAP. Falls back to an unweighted typical price when
                the bar volume disagrees with Tradier's session volume, which
                it frequently does.

  RSI           14-period, Wilder smoothing. OVERBOUGHT >= 70, OVERSOLD <= 30.
                Separately, a BAND reading: BULL_BAND is RSI 50-65 and rising,
                BEAR_BAND is 35-48 and falling. The band, not the extreme, is
                what the CLEAN tier uses.

  Bollinger     20-period, 2 standard deviations. Zone is UPPER_BAND /
                LOWER_BAND / NORMAL; a midline cross is recorded as CROSS_UP /
                CROSS_DOWN. The 20-period standard deviation is published as
                bollinger_sd and is the fallback for credit strike placement.

  ADX           Wilder ADX(14). TRENDING >= 22, otherwise CHOPPY. Recorded on
                every cycle and gates nothing - it was tested as a gate and did
                not earn one.

  Session move  Close against the regular-hours open, in percent. Published so
                a session-level trend filter could be tested; that filter is
                off (section 10).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW AN ENTRY IS DECIDED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An entry needs, in order: no kill switch, no daily-loss halt, no loss-streak
halt, a window open, a tier that fires, that window accepting that tier, the
direction allowed, the cooldown clear, and size greater than zero.

The tier ladder, most selective first. Only the first match applies.

  CLEAN     (enabled)  Four conditions agreeing. Bull: above the 20/50 EMAs,
                       EMA9 above EMA20, above VWAP, RSI in the bull band, and
                       macro GOOD. Bear: the mirror, without the macro
                       requirement.
  STRICT    (enabled)  MACD, trend, a Bollinger pierce AND an RSI extreme.
  RELAXED   (enabled)  STRICT without the RSI requirement.
  REJECT    (enabled)  A 50 EMA rejection with trend and VWAP already down.
                       Bearish only.
  MOMENTUM  (off)
  TREND     (off)

MORNING_DRIFT accepts CLEAN only, and long only. Its bear side was measured at
27% wins and -50.62 a trade, negative in both sample halves.

AFTERNOON_CREDIT accepts any tier, either direction: a bullish read sells puts
below spot, a bearish read sells calls above.

Entries are refused for 30 minutes on the side that just lost, and the whole
session stands down after 3 consecutive losses (AFTERNOON_CREDIT is exempt
from the streak halt; the dollar caps still bind it).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE MACRO GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Once a cycle, market_signals_agent builds a verdict from four inputs and asks
Claude for GOOD or BAD, plus a confidence and a written reason. Both are
logged so a flip can be explained afterwards rather than guessed at.

  - Advance/decline breadth across the Nasdaq-100, self-computed from Tradier
    quotes. Index breadth rather than full-market breadth, because QQQ tracks
    the index.
  - The VIX. At or above 22.0 the engine halts new entries in both directions -
    disorder is not directional.
  - Freshly scraped headlines.
  - The three nearest past headlines from pgvector (market_news_vectors,
    Voyage-4 embeddings, cosine distance).

market_sentiment GOOD is required for BULLISH entries only. A bearish setup
does not need it: the conditions that make being long uncomfortable are the
conditions a short position profits from.

Crude oil and the 10-year yield are fetched and logged every cycle and gate
nothing. Measured against the engine's own daily P&L, neither carries a usable
signal at decision time - both extremes beat the middle, in opposite
directions, which is the shape of noise. See section 10.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. HOW A POSITION EXITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Checked in this order, every cycle:

  FORCE CLOSE   15:45, regardless of P&L.

  If the window RIDES (MORNING_DRIFT):
    Ride ceiling  90% of the structure's maximum profit. A spread cannot be
                  worth more than its width, and inside the last tenth there is
                  no move left to ride - only convergence, held through the
                  widest quotes of the day. Expressed as a share of max profit
                  rather than a return, because the two differ per entry.
    Stop loss     -20% of premium paid.
    Handoff       13:25. The slot goes to the credit window.
    Otherwise     Hold. There is no take-profit and no ratchet on a ride;
                  both were tested and both cost money (section 10).

  Otherwise (AFTERNOON_CREDIT):
    Target        Above 50% of the credit collected the trail ARMS rather than
                  selling. Above 90% it books outright - the last tenth is the
                  worst-paid, since buying a spread back at 0.04 costs the same
                  two-legged spread as buying it at 0.20.
    Trail break   The 9 EMA turning against the structure, but ONLY when spot
                  is within $2 of the short strike. Direction alone does not
                  threaten short premium; distance to the strike does.
    Ratchet       Hands back more than 30% of its peak (minimum 10 points).
                  This is the exit that fires most often.
    Stop loss     -100% of the credit - the classic buy-it-back-at-twice rule.
    Risk-off      A tighter -13% stop for LONG positions while macro is BAD.
                  Never applied to shorts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. PRICING - WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Entries and marks come from Tradier's live option chain, with greeks. The
normal-CDF model in broker.py is the fallback when the chain is missing a
strike.

  Entry marks at the NATURAL price: a vertical sold fills at its bid, one
  bought fills at its ask.
  An open position marks at the MID - what it is worth, as opposed to what a
  fill costs.

This replaced model pricing on 2026-08-21 after one session of logging both
side by side. The model priced an open credit spread at 0.20-0.34 all
afternoon while the market quoted 0.01-0.04, an 88-97% overstatement of what
closing it cost, and the exit rules read that number.

Every cycle still logs both, as a "Chain vs model" line, so the gap stays
visible.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SIZING AND RISK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Position size is the smallest of four independent limits.

  1. Entry fraction     20% of equity, per trade (no window override is set;
                        the 8% MORNING_DRIFT override was removed). This is
                        capital deployed. 20%, not the 15% this file said
                        until 2026-08-29 -- verified against the container.
  2. Risk share         A window's share of the day's loss budget. Both live
                        windows: 50%. Sized so ONE stop-out cannot exceed it.
                        Post-loss re-entries use 30% and are halved.
  3. Tail cap           15% of equity at STRUCTURAL risk - premium paid for a
                        debit, width-minus-credit for a credit. This is the
                        only limit that governs what the market can impose
                        rather than what the rules intend, and it is usually
                        what binds.
  4. Minimum credit     A credit spread priced below $0.05 is refused outright.

Session-level circuit breakers:

  Daily loss cap        6% of the equity the session started with. Past it, no
                        new entries; open positions are still managed, because
                        refusing to manage what you hold is not risk control.
  Loss streak           3 consecutive losses stands the session down.
  Kill switch           KILL_SWITCH.txt halts everything.

Equity is realised: starting budget plus every closed trade. Size follows the
account down after losses and up after wins.

9. THE TWO LIVE WINDOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MORNING_DRIFT - 10:15 to 12:30, rides to the 13:25 handoff
  Structure     $10 wide, long leg $2 in the money, short leg $8 out. Bullish
                only, CLEAN tier only.
  Sizing        20% entry fraction (global, no window override), 50% risk share.
  Exits         -10% stop, 13:25 handoff, 15:45 force close. NO take-profit
                and no ratchet; the 90%-of-max ceiling exists but is
                unreachable (see below).

  WHAT THIS STRUCTURE ACTUALLY IS. Calling it a debit vertical implies the
  short leg does work. It does not. Measured on the live chain 2026-08-28:

      entry 10:35   call 719/729   legs  62 delta 2.955  /  10 delta 0.045

  The short leg sold for FOUR AND A HALF CENTS against a $2.93 debit -- 1.5%
  of the structure. And it sits beyond the day's reach: from the 10:15 price
  QQQ's maximum up-move has a median of $4.05 and reaches $8 on 8 of 60
  sessions, 13%.

      up-move from 10:15 reaches   $2 OTM 70%   $3 OTM 62%   $4 OTM 52%
                                   $5 OTM 37%   $6 OTM 28%   $8 OTM 13%

  So this is a CAPPED LONG CALL, not a premium-selling structure. Max return
  is +241%, the ride ceiling sits at 90% of that near +195%, and neither is
  reachable in a session whose median move is $4.15. The short leg's only job
  is to satisfy section 1's never-a-naked-leg rule at the lowest possible cost
  to the upside, and 4.5 cents is what that costs.

  Evidence      That the cap should stay out of reach is measured, not assumed.
                Per day with the credit window live, entry fraction 0.20,
                chain-priced, 60 sessions:

                    $10 wide, $2 ITM / $8 OTM  +34.49/day  worst -592.40
                    $8  wide, $2 ITM / $6 OTM  +26.71/day  worst -529.20
                    $10 wide, $3 ITM / $7 OTM  +17.76/day  worst -410.80
                    $8  wide, $3 ITM / $5 OTM  +17.44/day  worst -402.80
                    $6  wide, $2 ITM / $4 OTM   +5.36/day  worst -499.20
                    $5  wide, $2 ITM / $3 OTM   +4.96/day  worst -463.20
                    $10 wide, $5 ITM / $5 OTM   -5.60/day  worst -326.80
                    $5  wide, $3 ITM / $2 OTM   -6.35/day  worst -326.80
                    $8  wide, $5 ITM / $3 OTM   -7.62/day  worst -294.80
                    $6  wide, $5 ITM / $1 OTM  -14.79/day  worst -318.80

                Monotone in BOTH directions, exactly as section 24 found on a
                different harness: wider is better and a shallower long leg is
                better, and the live cell is the best of ten.

                Narrowing the spread to where the short leg earns real premium
                costs about $29.53 a day and buys a worst day $129 shallower
                (-463 against -592). That is a far closer call than the deeper
                variants -- $5 ITM placements lose money outright at every
                width -- and it is the same comfort-for-P&L trade as sections
                24, 30 and 32. The structure is uncapped BECAUSE trending
                sessions pay for the stop-outs, and a cap the day reaches 62%
                of the time truncates exactly those.

                THE FULLY-ITM VARIANT IS THE WORST OF ALL. $6 wide with the
                long $5 ITM and the short $1 OTM loses $14.79 a day. It is
                insulated from theta and capped at a level a $4.15 median move
                clears constantly: a high win rate with no upside to pay for
                the losses.

AFTERNOON_CREDIT - 13:30 to 15:00
  Structure     $4 wide. Short strike chosen by DELTA, targeting 0.20, from the
                live chain; volatility placement (3 standard deviations) is the
                fallback. Either direction.
  Sizing        15% entry fraction (global), 50% risk share.
  Exits         Arm at 50% of credit, book at 90%, 30% ratchet, -100% stop.
  Evidence      $4 is the only width whose sample halves are both positive:
                $1 -83.85/trade, $2 -13.17, $3 +26.88, $4 +32.50, $5 +17.16,
                $6 +11.25. Delta placement replaced sigma placement after the
                latter chose a 7-delta strike the market valued at $0.02.

10. TESTED AND REJECTED - DO NOT RE-PROPOSE WITHOUT NEW EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each of these was built, measured over 60 sessions, and left switched off. The
knob usually still exists.

  Put debit spreads on down days      27% wins, -50.62/trade, negative in both
                                      halves. Down moves reverse faster than
                                      they continue.
  Morning iron condors                Short strikes touched on 74-80% of
                                      sessions at $4 out. A volatility squeeze
                                      does not identify a quiet morning: the
                                      calmest third holds 26% against 18%.
  More trades per day                 2.3 trades/day earns half of what 1.3
                                      earns, and multiplies the worst day by
                                      eleven.
  A longer morning window             Entries to 13:25 take +21.58/trade down
                                      to +1.74.
  Trend-only credit entries           Enters 17 minutes earlier and collects
                                      more decay; 89% wins becomes 76%.
  A ratchet on the morning ride       Worse at every arm level from +12% to
                                      +32%.
  A fixed-dollar ratchet offset       The $0.25 equivalent is the worst variant
                                      tested. A share of peak widens as the
                                      position wins; a fixed offset does not.
  Refusing longs on a down session    Costs 7-9 dollars a day and does not
                                      improve the worst day by a cent.
  Uncapping on strong ADX             Worse at every threshold tested.
  A bear call spread on bad mornings  38% wins, -57.64 a trade, negative in
                                      both halves. Same cause as the put debit
                                      spread: morning declines reverse, which
                                      bleeds a structure needing continuation
                                      and runs over one needing the strike to
                                      hold. The engine waits and sells calls at
                                      13:30 instead, where the strike survives
                                      92% of sessions against 48% at 10:15.
  A tighter credit stop near the bell  Worse at every setting, and the worst
                                      day did not improve by a cent.
  A ratchet that tightens near the    Monotonically worse: +49.85 a day flat,
  structure's ceiling                 +47.56 / +33.45 / +30.89 as it tightens.
                                      Ratchet exits rise 16 -> 29 while target
                                      exits fall 14 -> 8, so it books winners
                                      that would have reached the target.
  A wider (or tighter) morning stop   No width wins. Widening lifts the win
                                      rate 36% -> 57% and cuts the worst day
                                      -971 -> -648, but per-trade P&L sags and
                                      recovers; tightening to -15% has the best
                                      average and halves of +74.70 then +5.34.
                                      Three columns, three answers, 24 trades.

WHERE THE LOSSES ACTUALLY COME FROM. Every one of the eight worst sessions
contains a MORNING_DRIFT stop-out, and four of them CLOSED UP (+9.04, +7.07,
+6.69, +3.99) -- entered, stopped on a pullback, and the day recovered without
it. Losing sessions average a SMALLER move than winning ones, 4.34 against
5.73, and stop-losses are 18 of 31 exits on losing days against 2 of 50 on
winning days. The tail is chop stopping out a directional position, it lives
in the morning window, and five separate exit-rule changes have failed to
touch it. Size is the only lever that has moved it.
  Yields and crude as gates           No usable signal at decision time.

Also open, and understood better than it was: a PUT DEBIT spread on
severely weak mornings only. Gating the short side on the session already
being down 0.75% takes it from -41.48 a trade to +166.52 and the engine from
+49.85 a day to +69.51 -- on six trades, with halves of +395.19 then -62.14.
Not enabled. The direction is what matters: on a severe decline the move
usually continues, so a debit structure monetises it while a bear call spread
caps at its credit no matter how far price falls. Severity lifts the bear
call's hit rate from 38% to 50% and never its economics.

A WEEKLY LAYER, observed and not traded. Every Friday at 15:45 the engine
marks three structures on the following week's expiry, at a 0.12-delta short
strike and $5 wide: a call credit spread, a put credit spread, and the condor
that is both. Nothing is ordered and no buying power is used. It exists
because weekly option prices are not available historically, so this cannot be
backtested at all -- the same wall the 0DTE condor hit.

Two measurements shaped it. Strikes come from DELTA, not dollars: "$2 out of
the money" is a 0.478 delta on a 5-day expiry, a coin flip, while 0.12 delta
sits $19-29 out. And the call side is the wrong side -- over 5 years of 5-day
outcomes the 0.12-delta call finished in the money 20.9% of the time against
the 11.7% the market charges, because delta is priced driftless and QQQ drifts
up. The put side is roughly fair at 12.3% against 11.4%. Crude EV per
contract: call -11.00, put +8.14.

Width is $5 on cost-adjusted economics. Raw return on risk favours narrow
spreads and inverts once crossing is charged: a $1-wide gives 83% of its
credit to the bid-ask, netting 2.3% on risk against 8.4% at $3-5.

Each row records BOTH outcomes -- when the spread first reaches the 25% target
that the Monday rule would book at, and what holding to expiry paid -- because
whether booking early beats sitting still is the open question and one number
cannot answer it.

Still open, with forward evidence accumulating: an iron condor at 13:30. It
beats either single side in every model-priced cell, but a condor sells four
far-OTM legs where a single spread sells two, so the model's known
overstatement flatters it roughly twice as much, and the four-leg bid-ask is
$9-11 against $2-15. The shadow condor logs market-priced marks at 09:45,
10:15 and 13:30, at two wing distances each, precisely to settle this.

Also open: VIX direction into 13:30 is the one macro input with a monotonic
relationship to the engine's P&L - falling +110.78/day at 68% green days,
rising -8.51 at 40%. Untested as a gate.

11. HOW TO CHANGE SOMETHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Run the sweep. scripts/sweep.py replays past sessions through the engine's own
agents and its own execution_risk_agent, with the clock, the data feed and the
database swapped out, so a result is what the deployed code would have done.

  python scripts/sweep.py windows      every window traded solo
  python scripts/sweep.py daily        trades per day against dollars per day
  python scripts/sweep.py size         risk share and entry fraction
  python scripts/sweep.py placement    morning width and depth, per day
  python scripts/sweep.py credit       credit widths
  python scripts/sweep.py breach       model-free strike survival rates
  python scripts/sweep.py condorvs     both sides against one side
  python scripts/sweep.py macro        yields, crude and VIX against P&L
  python scripts/sweep.py daytype      P&L by market regime

Read the module docstring before trusting a number. What the sweep cannot see:
macro sentiment (assumed GOOD on every session), real fills, and any session
containing a gap through a short strike - the 60-day sample has none, which is
exactly the risk the tail cap exists to bound.

What it DOES charge: 0.10 a contract of slippage and 0.014 of commission, both
per round trip. Commission is Tradier's $0.35 per contract per LEG, confirmed
against the account by scaling a preview order - a vertical is four legs a
round trip, so $1.40 a contract. It costs the deployed configuration about
$7.43 a day, roughly 15% of the measured edge, and it falls unevenly: about
17% of the credit window's per-trade result against almost nothing on the
morning window's. Charging it moves the engine from +49.85 a day to +42.42 and
turns the credit window's FIRST HALF negative standing alone, though the
combined configuration's halves stay level at +42.36/+42.48.

A parameter is worth changing when the winning cell wins in BOTH sample halves.
An average that reverses between halves is a result about the sample, not about
the parameter. Four of the last five ideas tested failed that bar.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. KNOWN LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Orders ARE routed now, to the live account, since 2026-08-25. What is
    still true: no resting stop exists at the exchange. Every exit is decided
    by a cron cycle that runs once a minute, so a gap through a short strike
    is bounded by the tail cap, by the one-contract order cap, and by nothing
    else. A minute is a long time in a 0DTE gamma move.
  - The credit window no longer earns most of the P&L; chain-priced it loses
    at every parameter tried (section 13). It cannot be validated by backtest
    either -- there are no historical option chains -- so its forward record
    is still the evidence that counts, and that record is now one real trade
    at -$23.
  - THE LLM MACRO TERM HAS NEVER BEEN IN ANY BACKTEST. sweep.py hardcodes
    market_sentiment="GOOD", so every number in this file was produced with
    that gate wired open. It was switched off live on 2026-08-25 so that the
    deployed engine and the measured engine are finally the same engine.
  - The chain-calibrated pricer has ONE anchor session for its volatility
    smile and one afternoon for its term structure. Absolute dollars from any
    sweep are indicative; the ranking between arms is the usable output.
    scripts/capture_chain.py collects the snapshots that would fix this.
  - trade_setup_vectors stores every closed trade's setup and outcome and is
    not consulted by anything. It waits for enough trades that "three similar
    setups all lost" is signal rather than noise.
  - The morning window's entire record is 24 backtested trades.
  - The live account is SHARED with manual trading. Positions in other
    underlyings appear in the broker's list and are not the engine's; the
    reconciliation is scoped to ENGINE_UNDERLYING so they are ignored. A
    manual QQQ position WOULD still be reported, and correctly so.
  - Log timestamps are UTC (the droplet is Etc/UTC) while every rule is
    America/New_York -- a four-hour offset in summer. See section 28.
  - There is no reachable take-profit on a riding position: the ride ceiling
    is 90% of MAX return, which on the deployed $10-wide is near +195%. The
    stop is the only rung that fires. See sections 28 and 29.

13. FIRST LIVE SESSIONS, 2026-08-24 and 2026-08-25
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Orders now route. TRADING_LIVE_ORDERS=true, TRADIER_ORDER_ENV=production,
account 6YB90697, TRADING_MAX_ORDER_CONTRACTS=1. Section 12's first known
limit is obsolete.

THE ORDER PATH WORKS, both directions. Open and close, multileg, correct leg
sides, correct OCC symbols, filled at the natural. Commission confirmed at
$0.35 a leg, so $1.40 a contract round trip -- the figure the sweeps have
been charging all along.

THE ONE LIVE TRADE, AND WHY IT LOST ON A DAY IT SHOULD HAVE WON

2026-08-25 was a falling session: open 713.12, close 709.49, net -$3.64. A
CALL credit spread is the structure for exactly that day. It lost anyway.

    14:34  sold QQQ 711/715 for 0.16   (spot 709.9, short strike ~0.20 delta)
    14:46  bought it back at 0.39      STOP_LOSS       -$23.00

QQQ's high for the whole rest of the session was 710.67, against a 711 short
strike. The spread was never breached and expired worthless. Held, it pays
+$14.60 after commission. The stop turned that into -$24.40, a $39 swing.

The stop fired on a 67-cent move in QQQ, because stop_loss_pct=-100 is
inherited from the debit side where it means "total loss of premium paid". On
a CREDIT position -100% means the spread DOUBLED, and doubling sixteen cents
is thirty-two cents. A credit spread's loss is already capped at
width-minus-credit by the long leg; the stop does not lower that ceiling, it
only converts unrealised noise into realised loss.

Swept through the engine, chain-priced, the stop level is worth real money
and does not rescue the window:

    stop -100% (live)   16% win   26/67 stopped   -47.14/tr   worst -1020
    stop -300%          19% win    6/59 stopped   -44.24/tr   worst -1020
    stop -400%          20% win    4/59 stopped   -36.52/tr   worst  -680
    stop -600%          22% win    3/59 stopped   -30.14/tr   worst  -340

A breach-based stop -- exit when spot actually reaches the short strike --
was tested and is WORSE than the percentage stop. A breached spread often
comes back, and stopping realises the loss at its worst moment.

THE MORNING NEVER TRADED, AND THE SWINGS WERE NOT TRADEABLE

10:15-12:30 on 2026-08-25 held a $2.17 range and five swings of a dollar or
more: +1.24, -1.32, +1.80, -1.16, +1.06. It is tempting to read that as five
missed trades. It is not. The engine-wide -20% stop on a $268 spread is about
$1.40 of QQQ movement, so every one of those swings sits INSIDE the noise
band the stop is drawn around. Caught perfectly at both ends the largest
would return roughly +16% of premium, about $43, less $11.40 of friction --

14. THE RISK/REWARD ARITHMETIC, AND TWO OUTSIDE NOTES TESTED 2026-08-25
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE FACT THAT EXPLAINS EVERY LOSING CREDIT VARIANT

A credit spread's break-even win rate IS its risk ratio, and delta IS the
market's estimate of the probability of finishing in the money. So a
15-delta short call has about an 85% chance of expiring worthless and pays
odds of exactly 85%. Measured across the delta sweep, $4 wide:

    delta  credit   risk   risk:reward  breakeven win%  actual win%   gap
     0.10  $  9.7  $ 390       40:1          97.6%          19%       -79
     0.15  $ 16.8  $ 383       23:1          95.8%          58%       -38
     0.20  $ 24.7  $ 375       15:1          93.8%          64%       -30
     0.30  $ 42.4  $ 358        8:1          89.4%          64%       -25
     0.40  $ 63.9  $ 336        5:1          84.0%          63%       -21

Selling closer narrows the gap and never closes it: the required win rate
falls faster than the achieved one rises. The structure is a fair bet before
costs and a losing one after, and $1.40 of commission is 13% of an $11
credit. Any edge has to come from implied vol exceeding realised, and at
0DTE the fee eats it first.

WHY PATH BEATS DIRECTION, which answers "QQQ falls most afternoons":

    59 sessions, 13:30 -> 15:45:  closed DOWN 56%, closed UP 44%
    but the max RISE above the 13:30 price before 15:45:
        rose >= $0.63 (0.40 delta strike)  78% of days
        rose >= $1.96 (0.20 delta strike)  29% of days
    median max-rise $1.12, against a median close of -$0.155

Direction is on the seller's side; path is not. Afternoons wander up before
drifting down, and a spread is marked against you on the way.

WIDTH: THE MARGINAL TABLE (0.15 delta, 13:00 entry, held to 15:45)

    width  credit  +credit    risk   +risk   ratio   net/tr
    $  1  $ 11.1       -    $  89      -     8.0:1   -3.22
    $  2  $ 16.2    +5.0    $ 184    +95    11.4:1   -3.77
    $  3  $ 18.2    +2.0    $ 282    +98    15.5:1   -5.32
    $  4  $ 18.9    +0.7    $ 381    +99    20.1:1   -7.03
    $  5  $ 19.2    +0.3    $ 481   +100    25.1:1   -8.63

Every extra dollar of width adds ~$99 of risk; the credit it adds runs
+5.00, +2.00, +0.70, +0.30. Past $2 the long leg is already worth a cent or
two, so widening removes protection without buying premium. The credit

BOOK THE FAST HALF AND STOP. RE-ENTRY IS WORSE.

    hold to 15:45, one trade      1.00 tr/day    -2.41/day
    book at 50%, no re-entry      1.00 tr/day    +1.40/day
    book at 50%, re-enter         3.32 tr/day   -10.80/day
    book at 30%, re-enter         4.10 tr/day   -21.78/day

Booking half the decay and releasing the collateral beats holding for the
slow half. Re-entering pays fixed friction against a premium that shrinks as
the session runs out. The one-position limit enforces this for free.

THE MORNING NOTE MEASURED BACKWARDS

An outside note recommended $2-3 wide morning debit spreads at +0.15 to
+0.25 net delta. Tested on the same CLEAN entries:

    OURS: $6 wide, long $2 ITM    35% win  net delta +0.60  debit/width 41%  +3.40/tr
    $3 wide, long 0.55 delta      32% win  net delta +0.34  debit/width 41%  +0.97/tr
    $2 wide, long 0.55 delta      32% win  net delta +0.23  debit/width 47%  -3.19/tr
    $2 wide, long 0.50 delta      28% win  net delta +0.23  debit/width 40%  -5.49/tr

Result improves monotonically with net delta, which matches this file's own

of width) is already satisfied at 41%.

The note applies "narrow is efficient" to both structures. It is right for

needs. Opposite structures, opposite answer.

the exits run off a once-a-minute cron with no resting order at the exchange,
so a failed closing order has no fallback.

STOPS GO THE OPPOSITE WAY FOR THE TWO STRUCTURES, and this is the part worth
keeping:

    MORNING debit spread, stop swept
        -20% (live)  +24.56/day    trades +162.58/tr  36% win
        -30%          -1.37/day           +90.55/tr   41% win
        -40%         -27.72/day           +35.89/tr   45% win
        -50%         -37.03/day           +14.63/tr   48% win

    AFTERNOON credit spread, stop swept
        -100%        -47.14/tr   26/67 stopped
        -300%        -44.24/tr    6/59
        -600%        -30.14/tr    3/59

Widening the stop helps the CREDIT spread and destroys the DEBIT one, and in
both cases the win rate moves the other way from the P&L. The reason is what
the stop is for in each structure. A debit spread's loss is already capped at
the premium paid, so its stop is not limiting risk -- it is rotating capital
out of a dead trade and into the next setup, and holding losers costs the
next entry. A credit spread's loss is capped by the long leg instead, so its
stop adds nothing and only converts unrealised noise into realised loss.

"Do not book losses early" is therefore right for one window and wrong for
the other. It is the single most counter-intuitive result in this file.

16. WHY "TRADE MORE" KEEPS LOSING  (2026-08-25, five independent tests)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The idea arrives in many forms -- book early, re-enter after a dip, ladder
the entry, ratchet out on a stall, keep the window open longer -- and every
one of them was measured. All five lose, and all five raise the WIN RATE
while lowering the P&L, which is what makes the idea so persistent:

    credit spread, book 50% then re-enter    -10.80/day  vs +1.40 one trade
    credit spread, book 30% then re-enter    -21.78/day
    morning, 3-tranche laddered entry        worse at every schedule tried
    morning, ratchet books on a fade          -36.61/tr  vs +294.41 riding
       (and a LOOSE ratchet does not fix it: 0.35 giveback -40.52/tr)
    morning, window open to 13:00             +161.08/tr vs +294.41 to 12:30
       -- 5 extra trades, -$2,262: the marginal entries average about -$290

One mechanism explains all of them. MORNING_DRIFT wins 39% of the time and
its entire edge is in the few trades that run. Any rule that books early or
trades more does two things at once: it pays the round-trip friction again
(~$11-14 a contract) and it truncates the winners that pay for the 61% of
losers. The win rate rises because small wins get banked; the P&L falls
because the large ones stop existing.

The counter-argument is real and priced: waiting does forfeit gains that were
briefly on the table. It is simply smaller than the cost of not being in the
trade on the days that run.

THE MORNING'S RATCHET IS DEAD CODE, deliberately. ride_to_close means only
the stop, the 13:25 deadline and the force close can end the trade -- no
target, no trail. That is not an oversight; enabling the ratchet was measured
at -36.61 a trade against +294.41 for riding.

WINDOW LENGTH IS A GENUINE OPTIMUM, not a default:

    entries until 11:30   20 tr  35% win  +202.94/tr  halves +712/-307
    entries until 12:30   23 tr  39% win  +294.41/tr  halves +629/ -12   <- live
    entries until 13:00   28 tr  36% win  +161.08/tr  halves +316/  +6

12:30 wins on total and nearly ties the best second half. Too short and it
misses setups; too long and it takes worse ones.

20. THE FIRST MORNING FILL, AND A CLOSE THAT NEVER CLOSED  (2026-08-27)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MORNING_DRIFT fired for the first time with real money, and the trade itself
is not the story. What matters is what happened to the exit.

    10:52  BULL via MORNING_DRIFT:CLEAN, 2 contracts, long 716 / short 726
           at $3.08 on $4,178 equity. macro=GOOD, the first all week.
           Order 143622161 filled both legs.
    11:02  An order the engine did not place bought back both 726 shorts.
    11:39  STOP_LOSS fired. The engine submitted its multileg close and
           Tradier answered {'id': 143647390, 'status': 'ok'}.
    11:40  That order was REJECTED -- it tried to buy back a short leg the
           account no longer held.

The engine booked the trade anyway. It wrote a TradeHistory row with a
realised P&L, deleted the OpenPosition row, and from the next cycle onward
logged "the engine believes it is flat". Two real long calls were left
unmanaged, ITM, four and a half hours from expiry.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG

Not the external order. The external order exposed it, but the defect stands
on its own: 'status': 'ok' from Tradier means ACCEPTED FOR PROCESSING, and
the engine read it as FILLED. service._route_order was written as a shadow
path -- deliberately, and correctly, back when it sent nothing -- with the
engine's own row authoritative and the broker's reply logged and discarded.
Nobody revisited that when LIVE_ORDERS went true. So every close since has
been booked on the assumption that submitting an order and completing one are
the same event, and any rejection at all -- a limit that never fills, a
halted underlying, a margin refusal -- would have silently abandoned the
position and written a fictitious P&L.

That last part is the expensive half. This repo judges every strategy on its
recorded results, and the record now contains at least one close that did not
happen. A backtest can be wrong and be fixed; a corrupted live record is what
the sweeps are calibrated against.

THE FIX

_close_rejected polls the order's terminal status before anything is booked.
On an explicit refusal -- rejected, canceled, expired, error -- the
OpenPosition row STAYS, no TradeHistory is written, and the next cycle sees a
live position and can act on it.

Only an explicit refusal counts. An order still working when the poll runs
out is treated as standing, because the opposite mistake is worse: a row kept
open on an order that then fills would have the next cycle sell a position
the account no longer holds. Between booking a close that is merely slow and
double-closing one that already went through, the slow case is the one
RECONCILE catches every cycle.

OPENS ARE STILL SHADOW. An open is written to the database whatever the
broker says. Same class of bug, not yet fixed, and it shows up as a RECONCILE
line rather than a corrected position.

THE OTHER PROBLEM, WHICH CODE CANNOT FIX

This account has more than one thing trading it. Alongside the engine's
position it held QQQ 714C +15, 718C -10, 719C -7 (which grew from -5 while
this was being investigated), plus MU and SNDK spreads. The engine did not
place any of them.

Three things it relies on are wrong as a result: the equity figure that sizes
every position, the 15% tail cap computed from that equity, and the leg
reconciliation. It sized 2 contracts against $4,178 of assumed equity while
other positions consumed margin it could not see.

No parameter in playbook.py accounts for a second decision-maker on the same
margin balance. The engine needs its own account, or a hard sub-allocation it
can treat as the whole world.

21. THE CREDIT FLOOR, MEASURED -- AND THE PRICE GRID THE HARNESS NEVER HAD
    (2026-08-27)

Live on 2026-08-27 the credit window sold 721/723 for 0.05 twice, and the

That prompted two things: a sweep of TRADING_MIN_CREDIT, and a correction to

THE HARNESS HAD NO PRICE GRID

broker.fill_price rounds to four decimals, so a replay could mark a spread at

pennies below $3.00 and nickels above, and every real exit lands there.

scripts/sweep.py now quantises simulated fills -- buys up a tick, sells down,
which is how a crossing order actually fills. SWEEP_TICK_PRICING=false
restores the old smooth behaviour for reproducing earlier numbers.

It matters least where premium is large and most where it is small, which is
exactly where this window lives:

    floor 0.05, smooth prices    -54.45/tr
    floor 0.05, penny grid       -63.56/tr      the grid costs $9 a trade

BUT IT DID NOT CHANGE ANY CONCLUSION, AND THE SWEEP WAS NEVER THE PROBLEM.
Both models say every floor loses. The smooth harness was flattering this
window by about 17% at a nickel and was already reporting it as a loser. The
live config was not chosen on a sweep that hid the failure -- the playbook's
own comment block says plainly that every afternoon premium-selling variant
measured on chain prices loses, and that "it is not a tuning problem".

THE FLOOR SWEEP, DEPLOYED CONFIG (13:30 start, $2 wing, 0.25 delta)
60 sessions, chain-priced, penny grid:

    floor   sessions traded   n    win%    per trade      total
    0.05          48/60      51    12%      -63.56       -3241.80
    0.10          41/60      43     9%      -45.80       -1969.60
    0.15          29/60      30    27%      -28.11        -843.40
    0.20          14/60      14    50%       -4.07          -57.00
    0.25           3/60       3    67%       +0.60           +1.80

READ THE SESSION COUNT, NOT THE AVERAGE. The per-trade number improves

Not a higher floor. Raising it to 0.20 turns a large loss into a small one by

brake on a vehicle pointed the wrong way.

The honest reading is the one section 14 reached from the probability side
and the playbook comment reached from the pricing side: selling market-priced
0DTE premium is a fair bet before costs, and there is no version of it that
survives them. This window should be off. Keeping it on at 0.20 is the
least-bad configuration if it stays, and it still loses.

23. CUTTING FASTER ON STRENGTH -- PROPOSED, MEASURED, REFUTED  (2026-08-27)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proposal: a bullish session is where a call credit spread gets hurt, so

percentage stop.

The premise is right about the day. 2026-08-27 closed +0.58%, the strongest
up-close in twelve sessions, and it finished at 721.11 against a 721.35 high.

And today it would have WORKED. The live 721/723 was ratcheted out at $0.00
and QQQ closed at 721.11, so holding to expiry settles the short at 0.11
against a 0.05 credit -- about -$18 on three contracts. Cutting saved money
on the day it was proposed.

Over 60 sessions it does not. Same strikes, same entry minute, same sessions,
exit rule the only variable, chain-priced on the penny grid:

    hold to close, no stop      93% win   +4.55/tr   halves  +4.87/+4.23
    stop at 6x credit (live)    87% win   -2.38/tr   halves  -2.13/-2.63

    stop at 2x credit           45% win   -8.32/tr   halves -10.97/-5.67

Every rule that cuts faster ranks below holding, and holding is the only arm
positive in both halves. The new-high rule fired 11 times in 60 sessions,
still lost, and its halves flip sign -- an unstable rule on a small count.

WHY, AND IT IS THE SAME REASON AS SECTION 15. A credit spread's loss is
ALREADY capped at width-minus-credit by the long leg. No exit rule lowers
that ceiling; it can only convert unrealised noise into realised loss earlier.
The tape making a new high is not information about where the spread settles
-- it is the noise the long leg was bought to absorb.

This is the third time the same shape of idea has been tested and refuted:
sell into strength (17), the upper-band fade (18), and now the strength exit.
All three read a bullish tape as a signal to act, and all three found that
the signal is already in the price.

THE HARNESS CAVEAT, WHICH MATTERS HERE. This arm holds one contract with no
ratchet and no re-entry, and it reports +4.55/tr for holding while the full
engine replay in section 21 reports the credit window losing at every floor.
They disagree on LEVEL and that disagreement is not resolved. What survives
is the RANKING, which is consistent and one-directional across all four
cutting rules. Rank entry and exit rules with this; never size the benefit.

WHAT IT ARGUES. The live -600% stop is the second-best arm and no stop is the
best. That is a live config question and not a sweep's to decide, but the
direction is clear: if the credit window stays on, it should be cut LESS, not
more.

29. THE WEEKLY BOOK LEARNS WHAT MARKET IT WAS OPENED INTO  (2026-08-28)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Asked: should the weekly single-name spreads be traded off the overall market
(QQQ), RV/IV, Bollinger, the moving averages and VWAP -- the way the 0DTE
engine reads a setup?

The book stays SHADOW ONLY. What changed is that the question became
answerable: every weekly_shadow row now records the market state it was
opened into, in sixteen sig_ columns. Nothing gates an entry; every structure
is still opened on every symbol.

WHY RECORDING RATHER THAN GATING. Weekly option prices have no historical
feed, so a gate cannot be backtested -- the same wall the 0DTE condor and
this book already hit. The rows as they stood held strikes, deltas and
credits and nothing about the market around them, so "would gating on trend
have helped" could not be asked even in hindsight. Eight to ten Fridays of
these columns makes it answerable against real marks, and each row already
records both outcomes -- the 25% target and hold-to-expiry.

THREE THINGS THE PROPOSAL GOT CHANGED ON THE WAY IN.

  VWAP IS NOT RECORDED. It is session-scoped and a weekly position spans five
  sessions and five VWAPs. Friday afternoon's value says nothing about the
  hold. It belongs to the 0DTE engine and does not transfer.

  DAILY BARS, NOT 5-MINUTE ONES. A 20-period Bollinger band on 5-minute bars
  describes ninety minutes; on a five-day hold that is noise. Same indicator
  names, different timescale, and the timescale is the point.

  THE CALL SIDE IS THE MEASURED-NEGATIVE WING, at least on QQQ: over 5 years
  of 5-day outcomes the 0.12-delta call finished ITM 20.9% against 11.7%
  priced (EV -11.00 a contract) while the put was 12.3% against 11.4% (EV
  +8.14). Delta is priced driftless and the index drifts up. Whether a single
  name shares that drift is open and per-name, which is why the wings are
  recorded separately. Nothing here commits to a side.

sig_rv_iv_ratio IS THE COLUMN THAT DECIDES WHETHER THE REST MATTERS. Above
1.0 the name realises more than it charges -- the wrong side of the variance
risk premium. It is stored PER VARIANT, not per symbol, because the call and
the put quote at different vols and one ratio would hide exactly the skew the
wings exist to expose. A rough run on 2026-08-27 put ten of eleven names above
1.0. If that survives period-matched measurement, no technical gate rescues
the structure: section 21 reached the identical conclusion about the credit
floor, which turned a large loss into a small one by trading a quarter as
often and never into a profit.

Labels AND numbers are stored, so a different threshold can be tested later
without waiting another ten Fridays to collect the data again.

A RESTART DOES NOT PICK UP AN ENV CHANGE

Section 24 recorded that `docker compose up -d` without both -f files
silently drops to paper mode, and that `restart` preserves configuration.
Both are true, and the second has a consequence that was not written down:
`restart` preserves the container's ORIGINAL environment, so it cannot apply
a new variable at all. Adding TRADING_RIDE_TAKE_PROFIT to .env.production and
restarting left the container without it, silently and with no error.

Applying an env change requires RECREATING the container, which means `up -d`
WITH both -f files:

    docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.prod.yml up -d app

Done after the close with no position open, and every value verified
afterwards rather than assumed: LIVE_ORDERS true, PREVIEW_ONLY false, stop
-10, width 10.0, credit start 14:00, floor 0.20, budget 4000, cap 3, and the
new rung at 50. A cycle was then run by hand and returned "skipped -- outside
market hours (Fri 16:13 ET)".

So: `restart` for a code change on the bind mount, `up -d` with both files
for an env change, and verify the whole table either way.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The architectural objection: the windows encode WHEN as a proxy for WHAT
CONDITIONS. A time-of-day rule standing in for a market-state rule is crude.
Let the signal decide direction and let it trade whenever it is valid, with
risk controls instead of clock controls.

The specific form of the claim is what made it worth testing: each
restriction is individually defensible, but COLLECTIVELY they are too tight.
Every earlier sweep in this file moved one knob, so the combination had never
been run, and the claim is precisely about the combination.

Per day, both windows, 60 sessions, chain-priced, at the deployed
configuration including the +50% ride rung:

    deployed (clock-driven)      +18.47/day  0.77 tr/day  23% green
      morning: ALL tiers         -12.96/day  0.90 tr/day
      morning: both directions   -27.25/day  1.42 tr/day
      morning: opens 09:45       -48.59/day  0.97 tr/day
      credit:  opens 11:00        +2.96/day  1.43 tr/day  42% green
    COMBINED
      tiers + directions        -107.21/day  1.85 tr/day
      FULLY RELAXED (all four)  -134.30/day  2.73 tr/day

THE CLAIM IS REFUTED, AND BACKWARDS. The restrictions do MORE work together
than apart, not less:

    removing tiers alone costs        -31.43/day
    removing directions alone costs   -45.72/day
                        additive      -77.15   predicts  -58.68/day
                        actual                           -107.21/day
                        interaction   -48.53/day of damage that is not
                                      explained by either change alone

THE MECHANISM IS THE SINGLE POSITION SLOT. With all tiers and both
directions the morning fires early and often -- 1.85 trades a day against
0.77 -- and holds the one slot until the 13:25 handoff. The credit window,
the only component that survives relaxation on its own, is then locked out by
a morning trade a stricter engine would never have opened. The restrictions
are not independent filters each earning its place; they are jointly
protecting a scarce resource, and that is why they cannot be evaluated one at
a time.

This is the first interaction effect measured in this file, and it is only
visible because the components were run together. Every parameter here was
chosen by moving one knob, which is the right way to attribute a change and
the wrong way to evaluate a design.

WORTH NOTING IN THE PROPOSAL'S FAVOUR. `credit opens 11:00` posts 42% green
days against 23% deployed while earning a fifth as much. More winning days,
less money -- the same comfort-bought-with-P&L shape as sections 16, 24 and
30. If green-day frequency is worth something independently of return, that
is the row that offers it, and it costs about 15 dollars a day.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE CODE, AND WHAT DID NOT. Nothing live moved. What moved is
that the restrictions are now testable from the environment rather than only
by editing code: TRADING_MORNING_TIERS (list, or ALL),
TRADING_MORNING_BULLISH_ONLY and TRADING_MORNING_CREDIT_BEARISH_ONLY join the
times and widths that were already configurable. The asymmetry mattered --
knobs that were cheap to sweep got swept repeatedly, and knobs that needed a
redeploy were defended by their own inaccessibility.

IRON CONDORS AND SINGLE NAMES: THE SAME RULES CANNOT BE APPLIED YET, and the
reason is measurement rather than reluctance. The 0DTE condor cannot be
backtested at all -- broker.py's model overstates far-OTM premium and a
condor sells four such legs where a vertical sells two, so the flattery is
roughly doubled, on top of a four-leg bid-ask of $9-11 against $2-15. Weekly
single names have no historical option feed whatsoever. Both are on forward
marks, and section 29's signal columns are what will make the single-name
version answerable in eight to ten Fridays.

A prior question outranks the entry rules there: the rough run put realised
vol ABOVE implied on ten of eleven names. If that survives period-matched
measurement, no entry rule rescues those structures, and applying good rules
to a negative-edge trade is what section 21 already caught the credit floor
doing.

34. THE WEEKLY BOOK -- A PROPOSED STRATEGY, AND WHAT BACKS EACH PARAMETER
    (2026-08-28)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two books, at completely different evidence stages. They look like one and
must not be run as one: the QQQ edge is measured over five years, and merging
them lets it underwrite eleven names that have earned nothing.

Every parameter below carries its evidence, or says plainly that it has none.

================================================================================
A. QQQ WEEKLY PUT CREDIT SPREAD -- proposable now
================================================================================

STRUCTURE      Put credit spread. Sell the 0.12-delta put, buy $5 lower.
               Never a call spread. Never a condor.

  EVIDENCE     Five years of 5-day outcomes on QQQ:
                   0.12-delta CALL  finished ITM 20.9%  priced 11.7%  EV -11.00
                   0.12-delta PUT   finished ITM 12.3%  priced 11.4%  EV  +8.14
               Delta is priced driftless and QQQ drifts up, so the call seller
               is short the index's own direction. A condor deliberately pairs
               the +8.14 wing with the -11.00 one; it is two trades, one good
               and one bad, reported as a single number.

STRIKE         By DELTA, not by dollars. 0.12.
  EVIDENCE     "$2 out of the money" on a 5-day expiry measured 0.478 delta
               with QQQ at 713.41 -- a coin flip on a 1:1 payoff. The
               0.12-delta strike sat $19-29 out. Also: over 150 weekends QQQ
               gapped 2+ dollars on 32.7%, 4+ on 19.3%, 7+ on 8.0%, measured
               when QQQ averaged $517 and so about 38% larger now. A near
               strike is gapped through while the position cannot be managed
               for sixty-five hours.

WIDTH          $5, and at least one strike.
  EVIDENCE     Cost-adjusted return on risk. Raw return favours narrow and
               inverts once crossing is charged: $1-wide gives 83% of its
               credit to the bid-ask, netting 2.3% on risk against 8.4% at
               $3-5. _strike_spacing reads the grid from the chain, so $5 is
               five strikes on a $1 name and one on a $5 name.

ENTRY          Friday 15:45 ET, next week's expiry.
  EVIDENCE     None on the timing itself. It is where the shadow record was
               collected, so it is the only entry the forward data describes.
               Do not read it as measured.

FLOOR          Skip if the natural credit is below $0.10.
  EVIDENCE     Direct: on the 2026-08-28 dry run STX and WDC priced at zero
               natural credit -- the 0.12-delta wing had no bid -- and the
               floor skipped them. Refusing a quote that does not exist, which
               is a different job from the 0DTE floor, where no floor could
               rescue a structure with no edge (section 21).

EXIT           OPEN QUESTION, and the one thing to resolve first. Every shadow
               row records BOTH the 25%-of-credit target and the
               hold-to-expiry outcome precisely because one number cannot
               answer it. Do not deploy an exit rule until those rows disagree
               or agree with a margin.

SIZING         Not proposed. Weekend gap risk is the binding constraint and it
               has not been sized against the 0DTE book's tail cap. This is
               the gap that must close before real money.

WHY THIS IS STILL A PROPOSAL. The EV figures are per-contract crude numbers
over 5-day outcomes; they are not a backtest of this strategy, because weekly
option prices have no historical feed. What is measured is the ASYMMETRY
between the wings and the width economics. What is not measured is what the
whole thing earns.

================================================================================
B. SINGLE NAMES -- NOT proposable, and here is the precondition
================================================================================

No strategy is proposed for MU, SNDK, CRWV, STX, WDC, DELL, META, MSFT,
GOOGL, AMZN or MRVL. Writing one now would be inventing rules and citing
QQQ's evidence for names that have not earned it.

THE PRECONDITION, IN ORDER:

  1. rv_vs_iv.py must report implied ABOVE realised, per name, period-matched.
     A rough run on 2026-08-27 put ten of eleven names the WRONG way, ratios
     1.17 to 1.93, breach rates 36-60% against the ~30% a 0.15-delta
     structure expects. That comparison was unsound -- one day's implied
     against six months of realised, with an earnings gap inside the window --
     which is why the period-matched version exists. First pairs land early
     September.

     If it confirms realised above implied, NOTHING ELSE MATTERS. Selling
     market-priced options is a fair bet before costs and a losing one after;
     no entry gate, no indicator and no structure rescues it. Section 21
     reached this conclusion about the 0DTE credit floor and it cost weeks of
     tuning to learn.

  2. The call/put asymmetry must be measured PER NAME. QQQ's 20.9%-against-
     11.7% comes from index drift. A high-IV single stock has no reason to
     share it, and the sign could invert. weekly_shadow records the wings
     separately for exactly this; collapsing them into a condor would hide the
     effect worth knowing about.

  3. Only then do the entry gates become a question. Section 29's sig_
     columns began recording 2026-08-28, so eight to ten Fridays of trend,
     Bollinger, RSI, 5-day move and per-variant RV/IV exist around
     mid-November.

  4. EARNINGS. Not yet handled anywhere. A weekly hold over a single name's
     earnings date is a different trade, and the 5-day gap statistics that
     justify the 0.12 delta on QQQ do not describe it.

WHAT TO DO IN THE MEANTIME: nothing new. The shadow book already marks CALL,
PUT and CONDOR on every name every Friday and costs nothing. The order is
deliberate and is the one thing this file has learned twice -- the 0DTE
credit window ran live for weeks before its own numbers said it loses at
every setting, and that is the mistake not to repeat on eleven names at once.

38. THE STRUCTURE IS CHOSEN BY THE STOP, NOT BY THE DAILY RANGE  (2026-08-29)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The objection: QQQ moves $4-7 a day, so a $10-wide spread with the short leg
$8 out asks for a move that never happens, and the structure should fit the
range. The premise is exactly right. The inference is a win-rate rule where
the engine needs an expected-value one.

THE GEOMETRY, MEASURED. 60 sessions, spot 715-725:

    |close - open|            median $4.15   mean $5.61   p75 $7.78
    max UP move from 10:15    median $4.05   p75 $6.23    p90 $8.88
    max UP move from 14:00    median $1.31   p75 $2.35    p90 $4.30

    a short call struck this far out is touched, from 10:15 / from 14:00:
      $2 OTM  70% / 32%     $4 OTM  52% / 10%     $6 OTM  28% /  7%
      $3 OTM  62% / 20%     $5 OTM  37% /  7%     $8 OTM  13% /  3%

So the live short leg is beyond the day's reach 87% of the time, which is why
it priced at 4.5 cents on 2026-08-28 (62 delta long 2.955, 10 delta short
0.045) against a $2.93 debit. The structure is a CAPPED LONG CALL and the
notes were wrong to imply the short leg does work. Section 9 now says so.

WHY WIDE STILL WINS, AND IT IS THE STOP THAT DECIDES

    $10 wide, $2 ITM, debit ~2.93:  max loss ~0.29 (the -10% stop)
                                    max gain  7.07 (width minus debit)
    $5  wide, $2 ITM, debit ~2.20:  max loss ~0.22
                                    max gain  2.80

Halving the width cuts the upside 60% and the downside by almost nothing,
because the downside is governed by the STOP and not by the width. The move
distribution is right-skewed -- median 4.05, p90 8.88 -- and a cap at $3
deletes that tail.

    THE RULE IS NOT "width should fit the median move". IT IS: the short leg
    belongs beyond where the distribution has mass, BECAUSE the stop already
    bounds the loss. Width buys right-tail exposure at nearly no left-tail
    cost.

AND THAT HOLDS ONLY WHILE THE STOP IS TIGHT. At a -40% or -50% stop the loss
scales with the debit again, the left tail reopens, and narrow spreads start
to make sense. The structure and the stop are ONE decision. Nothing in this
file recorded that dependency before now, and every width result in it was
produced at -10% or -20%.

40. LONG STRADDLES -- BUILT, MEASURED, REFUTED BY THEIR OWN BREAK-EVEN
    (2026-08-29)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both outside notes proposed buying volatility, and a grep for "straddle" or
"strangle" across the repository returned nothing. Every structure here is a
vertical, so the engine can express a DIRECTION and cannot express "a large
move, either way".

That gap looked important because of where the losses live: section 33 put
the entire drag in REVERSAL sessions, exactly the days a directional debit
spread is punished and a structure indifferent to sign is not.

Built as a measurement only -- sweep_straddle, priced through chain_pricer's
fitted surface, charged the same slippage and commission a vertical pays,
since both cross two legs each way. Nothing touches the live engine: a
straddle is not a vertical, and the position model, the order path and the
sizing rule would all need changing before one could be traded.

Twelve arms, 60 sessions, one contract:

    enter 10:15, hold to close   23% win  -213.11/tr   avg debit 7.24
    enter 10:15, +50% / -40%     23% win  -155.19/tr
    enter 10:15, +100% / -40%    22% win  -137.38/tr
    enter 10:15, +50% / -25%     17% win  -112.13/tr
    enter 09:45, +50% / -25%     22% win   -88.47/tr   avg debit 7.57
    enter 13:30, +50% / -25%     10% win   -99.23/tr   avg debit 4.64

EVERY ARM LOSES, IN BOTH HALVES, AT EVERY ENTRY TIME AND EVERY EXIT RULE.

THE REASON IS ARITHMETIC AND IT USES THIS FILE'S OWN GEOMETRY. An ATM
straddle at 10:15 costs $7.24. That is the break-even move, either direction,
by the close. Section 38 measured the distribution:

    max move from 10:15    median $4.05    p75 $6.23    p90 $8.88

The break-even sits above the 75th percentile. A p85 session is needed merely
to get the money back, before costs. The straddle is priced CORRECTLY --
implied vol is tracking the real distribution -- so buying it at fair value
loses the bid-ask every day, mechanically.

THE SYMMETRY WITH SECTIONS 14 AND 21 IS THE POINT. Selling market-priced
premium is a fair bet that loses after costs. BUYING market-priced premium is
also a fair bet that loses after costs. Both sides pay the spread. Whatever
edge this engine has does not come from an opinion about volatility; it comes
from a directional debit spread whose short leg subsidises 1.5% of the cost.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TEST. Both notes proposed straddles specifically around
scheduled events, and the sample holds two FOMC days. The event-specific
version cannot be measured here. For it to work, event days would have to
differ enormously from a -137 a trade baseline against a $7.24 break-even --
which is possible and is not evidence.

The sweep stays in the tree. If enough event days accumulate, the arm exists.

  * Engine polling is the ONLY exit mechanism this account has. There is no

  * Section 12's known limit is now a broker constraint rather than a to-do,

  * The morning window's gap exposure is bounded by STRUCTURE rather than by
    any order: a debit spread cannot lose more than its premium. Friday risked
    $586 in total across two contracts, and a gap to zero loses exactly that.
  * The credit window is where gap risk is genuinely open -- width minus
    credit, roughly $380 a contract against $20 of premium -- and it now has
    no exchange-side protection available even in principle. The tail cap is
    the only control.

EXIT POLLING SET TO 10 SECONDS. From Friday's worst minute the spread fell
30.6 points in 60 seconds, -0.51 a second:

    check every 60s  ->  up to 30.6 points of overshoot   (what ran all week)
    check every 15s  ->  up to  7.7 points
    check every 10s  ->  up to  5.1 points   <- deployed
    check every  5s  ->  up to  2.5 points

rather than the stop price even if one were available. Gap protection here is
structure and size, in that order, and never the exit rule.

THE ONE THING IT NAMES THAT GENUINELY DOES NOT EXIST: PARTIAL SCALING.

Every exit in this engine is all-or-nothing. broker.sell_all() is the only
close path, and the OpenPosition row carries a single quantity. Partial ENTRY
exists -- entry_tranche_qty and entry_slices_remaining -- but there is no
partial exit anywhere.

TO BUILD IT would need: a partial close on the broker path, remaining-quantity
tracking on the position row, per-tranche cost basis so the second exit's
return is computed against the right debit, and an order path that submits a

distribution has a fat middle to harvest. This one is bimodal: of 29 morning

of those six at +30% clips the only trades that pay, and sells half of
nothing on the 19 that never get there.

THE VARIANT ACTUALLY WORTH TESTING is not the note's. Scale at a HIGH level:
sell half at +100% and let the remainder ride to the handoff. That is the only
version that does not truncate the population carrying the strategy, and it is
untestable today because the mechanism does not exist. Left as the next
mechanism to build if the forward record justifies it.

THE PATTERN ACROSS ALL THREE OUTSIDE NOTES. Their OBSERVATIONS keep being
sound -- QQQ does move $4-7 a day, it does drift down after 13:00, the $8 OTM
short leg is nearly worthless, 0DTE does swing violently. Their PRESCRIPTIONS
keep failing here, and almost always for the same reason: they assume a naked
or ATM long with negative theta, a credit spread whose loss is not already
capped, or a return distribution with a middle. This engine is a capped long
call with a tight stop plus a two-sided credit window worth about a dollar a
day. Generic 0DTE advice is written for neither.

breaches the stop the trade closes AT THE STOP LEVEL. Optimistic bound on

the same losses smaller; it creates NEW stop-outs from positions the slow stop

was accidentally functioning as a much wider stop, and the strategy was

50. EV BY DELTA IS THE WRONG CALCULATION, AND WHAT IT COST  (2026-09-01)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Asked which structure to trade on a single name. Answering it turned up an
error in the method, not just in the answer.

THE WRONG CALCULATION. A credit spread's EV was computed as

    EV = credit - width x (delta_short + delta_long) / 2

using DELTA as the probability of breach. Delta is the RISK-NEUTRAL
probability -- derived from implied vol. The entire reason to sell premium is
that implied exceeds realised, so pricing the risk off delta assumes away the
edge before the sum begins. Every structure on every name at every strike
came out negative, including the QQQ weekly put that had just been enabled.

THE RIGHT ONE uses realised vol. A 0.12-delta strike sits about 1.17 implied
standard deviations out; at RV/IV 0.78 that is 1.17/0.78 = 1.50 REALISED
standard deviations, which is a materially smaller breach probability:

                        P(breach) by delta   P(breach) realised   EV delta   EV realised
    QQQ  0.20 x2              0.195               0.135              -14         -2
    CRWV 0.20 x2              0.188               0.027               -5        +22
    CRWV 0.30 x2              0.299               0.126               -5        +29

QQQ IS ROUGHLY BREAKEVEN AT EVERY STRIKE even crediting the full variance
risk premium -- -2 to -11 a contract, about what crossing costs. That is
sections 14 and 21 reproduced from live chains rather than from a backtest.
CRWV IS POSITIVE, and only because its implied is more than double its
realised.

A RETRACTION. On the strength of the delta calculation this file briefly
recorded that section 22's "+8.14 a contract" contradicted its own statistic.
That was overstated: the framing was wrong, not necessarily the figure. What
still stands is narrower -- the statistic quoted beside it (put finished ITM
12.3% against 11.4% priced) describes realised EXCEEDING implied, which is
the opposite of a variance risk premium and cannot by itself produce +8.14.
The figure is derived nowhere in the repository. It should not be cited until
it is.

60. THREE SILENT DEFECTS BEFORE THE OPEN, AND THE FINAL ORPHAN LADDER  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Orphan management was deployed the previous evening. All three defects below

BEEN SILENT: the logs would have looked ordinary while nothing was managed.

and "null" is a truthy string, so it raised AttributeError, was caught, and

Three spreads held overnight rebuilt to nothing -- "structures: 0" -- because
there were no opening fills in today's window. /history does reach prior days
and cannot fix it: it is per-LEG, so it can verify a price but cannot recover
which legs were PAIRED, which is the one thing the reconstruction needs.

account still holds both legs. Today's orders always win over the cache, so a
scale-in or partial close corrects it. Positions predating the cache were
seeded by hand after verifying every entry against /history:

    QQQ  buy 700 @ 9.69   sell 710 @ 2.20    ->  7.49   x1

all assume hours, not days. Scoped by name, the force close would have
flattened a Friday NVDA position on Wednesday afternoon for no reason.
ORPHAN_TODAY_ONLY scopes management to contracts expiring in the current New
York session, so a name is managed on its expiry day and watched before it,
with no setting to change in between. Compared against the NEW YORK date: after
20:00 ET the NY and UTC dates disagree and the disagreement would silently move
positions in and out of scope.

THE LADDER AS IT STANDS, in priority order. Stop first, because a position
already down 25% should not wait five minutes for a quiet-period test. Force
close second, because these settle in SHARES and not cash.

    STOP          -25% debit / -600% credit
    FORCE_CLOSE   15:45
    CEILING       90% of the structure's maximum
    STALL         5 minutes with no new high AND 3.3 points off the peak

25-cent giveback, flatten at 15:45.

65. TWO SCHEDULERS, A GUARDED AGENT, AND A DEPLOY THAT DID NOT DEPLOY  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A NEWS LOOKUP COULD HALT EXECUTION, AND NOW CANNOT.

market_signals_agent made two unguarded Anthropic calls while every other
network call in it -- crude, VIX, yields, breadth -- was wrapped. The first
took down three whole cycles at the open (section 64). Both are now guarded.

The headline read degrades to no headlines: it is observational, and the
verdict then rests on breadth, VIX and yields, which is where this file's
evidence says the signal lives anyway.

The sentiment call degrades to BAD, and the asymmetry is the point. The gate
reads `llm_verdict == "GOOD" or not MACRO_LLM_GATE`. With the gate OFF -- its
deployed state -- the value is inert and the fallback costs nothing. With the
gate ON, an unknown macro read must not PERMIT a bullish entry: refusing on an
outage is recoverable, entering on one is not. BAD is safe in BOTH
configurations, which is the property to want in a fallback nobody will
re-examine for months. It is deliberately not written to the macro cache,
since persisting a verdict the model never gave would suppress the retry for
the cache's whole life.

THERE ARE TWO THINGS THAT CAN RUN THE TRADING CYCLE.

The cron entry, every minute, and an in-app scheduler behind
POST /trading/scheduler/start. They do the SAME JOB. Run both and every cycle
happens twice, concurrently, with no locking -- and two cycles that both see a
position can both act on it.

Measured today: exactly one cycle a minute, all from cron, zero from the app
container. The in-app scheduler had been started manually and was killed by a
container recreate. No harm done, and the hazard is real: the QQQ 714/716
closing as two separate five-lot orders rather than one ten-lot (section 64,
defect 5) has exactly the shape a race would produce. NOT PROVEN -- the
timestamps were gone by the time it was noticed -- but it is the leading
explanation and the reason cron should be the only runner.

A DEPLOY THAT COPIED THE FILE AND CHANGED NOTHING.

`docker compose up -d` recreates a container only when its CONFIG changes.
Every earlier deploy today also edited .env.production, so each one recreated
and reloaded. The endpoint deploy copied files only -- up -d reported
"Started", the file on disk hash-matched the container, and the route still
404'd because uvicorn never reloaded.

FOR A CODE-ONLY DEPLOY, `restart` IS REQUIRED. `up -d` is not a no-op that
happens to also restart; it restarts as a side effect of recreating, and a
code-only change gives it no reason to recreate.

GET /trading/positions ADDED. /trading/position reports the engine's own
OpenPosition row and knows nothing about a position a human opened -- today it
would have shown nothing while eight structures were live. The new endpoint
reads the same reconstruction the cycle runs, and each row reports only the
rules that GOVERN it: stop_pct is null on a later expiry rather than quoting a
number that cannot fire, and the stall fields report whichever of the two
stalls applies. Read-only: the exit rules run in the cycle, not in a request
handler.

15:48 and a new position opened at 16:24 on different strikes. The

close. Recorded because "the basis looks wrong" is the right instinct after

67. THE MARK IS NOT THE POSITION  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The single most expensive misunderstanding in this file, found and fixed in
one afternoon, and it cost about 1,250 dollars in between.

AN IN-THE-MONEY SPREAD MARKS FAR BELOW WHAT IT IS WORTH. The short leg sits
nearer the money than the long, so it carries more time premium, and that
premium is SUBTRACTED from the spread. The gap goes to zero at expiry. It is
not a loss; it is the position not having expired yet.

Live, an hour before the close:

    sym   strikes        entry   mark     intrinsic   held to expiry
    AVGO  365/355 P       7.88   6.45      10.00        +26.9%
    SNDK  1500/1540 C    24.05  27.70      40.00        +66.3%
    DELL  530/520 P       6.28   6.75      10.00        +59.2%

AVGO read -18% on the mark while holding its FULL width in intrinsic. Every
one of eight live positions was in the money and above its entry, and five of
them showed a negative return.

TWO RULES WERE READING THE WRONG NUMBER.

THE STOP. Tomorrow AVGO becomes 0DTE and the -25% stop goes live at a mark of
5.91. One tick books -190 on a structure worth +212 at expiry -- and the
underlying had moved IN ITS FAVOUR to get there, falling through the short
strike. Fixed first: the stop is suppressed when intrinsic exceeds what was
paid.

THE STALL, WHICH WAS THE EXPENSIVE HALF AND WAS FIXED SECOND. The stop guard
was built and I stopped there. That was the error. The stop only fires on
LOSERS; the stall fires on WINNERS, which is exactly where surrendering
intrinsic costs the most. Within the hour:

    17:20  ORPHAN STALL_LATER: closing SNDK 1500/1540 x1 -> booked +345

Entry 24.05, sold near 27.50, SNDK at 1558 and above both strikes. The spread
held 40.00 of intrinsic and pays +1,595 held to expiry. The stall read a mark
fading from time premium and called it a profit that had stopped climbing. It
was the profit ARRIVING. MU closed on the same rule eight minutes earlier.

The stall now tracks its peak and giveback on INTRINSIC versus entry, falling
back to the mark only when intrinsic cannot be computed. It is not weakened: a
genuine reversal shrinks intrinsic and still books the trade. Only decay stops
triggering it.

WHAT THE ATTRIBUTION SHOWS, and why it was added. Every ORPHAN line and the
/trading/positions response now carry intrinsic and extrinsic beside the
value. A negative extrinsic on an ITM spread is the part that comes back; a
bare percentage cannot tell that from a real adverse move, and both rules
above were built on the bare percentage.

AND THE ACCOUNT-LEVEL VERSION OF THE SAME MISTAKE. The account marked 15,530
against 16,000 earlier and the question was whether to guard total value. It
should not be guarded on marks. The book held 4,214 of extrinsic due back and
was +2,298 above cost held to expiry at that day's spots, while showing -3,183
unrealised. An account-value stop would have liquidated all of it at once, at
whatever the book offered -- the same error as the position rules, scaled up
and made simultaneous.

The existing 6% daily limit counts REALISED P&L and halts entries only. That
is the correct shape. It was nowhere near firing: realised on the day was
+2,235.

THE GENERAL RULE, worth carrying past this engine: A RULE EXPRESSED AS A
RETURN MUST NAME WHICH VALUE IT MEANS. Mark, intrinsic, and value-at-expiry
are three different series, they diverge most exactly when a position is
working, and every exit rule in this file was written against the first
without ever saying so.

68. LEGGED-IN SPREADS, AND THE FIRST FULL DAY OF ORPHAN MANAGEMENT  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PAIRING BY ORDER MISSES A SPREAD LEGGED INTO ACROSS ORDERS.

Order-based pairing recovers the structure a human actually put on, which is
what makes the entry prices trustworthy. It cannot recover one assembled from
separate orders: a SNDK 1500 bought on 09-02 and a 1530 sold at 14:14 on 09-03
are economically a spread and look to the pairer like two unrelated singles.
Two legs sat outside the ladder entirely -- reported as ORPHAN SINGLE, never
marked, never stopped, never stalled.

The fallback pairs leftovers by strike within one underlying, expiry and right,
priced from tradier_orders.filled_legs rather than Tradier's averaged
cost_basis. A pair with no recoverable fill is skipped: a return computed
without a true entry is not worth acting on, and every rule here is a return.

INFERRED PAIRS ARE REPORTED, NOT ACTED ON, unless TRADING_ORPHAN_MANAGE_INFERRED
is set. Pairing by strike is a GUESS -- the same guess that made the engine's
naked-leg check read a fully covered book as naked earlier the same day. It is
usually right and it is not stated fact.

THE FIRST VERSION OF THE FALLBACK FOUND NOTHING, and the reason is worth
recording. It treated a symbol as consumed if it appeared in ANY stated pair,
so a SNDK 1500 held TWICE, with one contract inside a stated 1500/1540, left
the second contract invisible and the 1530 short with nothing to pair against.
Leftover is what the account HOLDS minus what the stated structures USE.
Coverage went 15 of 17 legs to 16.

The recovered entry is also the second demonstration that fills beat cost
basis: 24.63 from the real fills (50.25 against 25.62), where the averaged
cost_basis implies 35.78 -- ABOVE the structure's own 30-dollar width, which
would have made the position look structurally impossible.

The one leg still uncovered is a NVDA 222.5 long x5. It is correctly
unpaired: there is no spare short above it, so there is no width, and every
rule here is a return against a width. Its risk is capped at the premium.

69. A ROLL PRODUCES AN UNMANAGED POSITION BY CONSTRUCTION  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rolling a short strike leaves the new structure INFERRED, and inferred pairs
were report-only. So the act of managing a position by hand removed it from
the engine's management.

    18:03  buy_to_close SNDK 1530 @ 47.03, sell_to_open SNDK 1540 @ 40.08

One order, a 6.95 debit. It closed one short and opened another, so the new
1540 short has NO OPENING ORDER tying it to a 1500 long. The pairer sees a
leftover long and a leftover short, pairs them by strike, and marks the result
inferred -- correctly, since strike pairing is a guess. That guess was then
excluded from management by default, leaving the largest position in the book
(+1,924 at expiry) with no ceiling, no stall and no stop.

TRADING_ORPHAN_MANAGE_INFERRED is now true. The reasoning that made it default
false still holds -- pairing by strike is the guess that misread a covered
book as naked earlier the same day -- but a ROLL is the ordinary case, not an
edge case, and leaving rolled positions unmanaged is worse than acting on a
pairing that is obviously right.

THE ENTRY AFTER A ROLL IS 20.76, AND THAT IS CORRECT.

I first called this wrong. It is not. The reconstruction pairs the 1500 fill
(60.84) with the 1540 fill (40.08) and reports 20.76, ignoring the 21.41 lost
on the 1530 round trip (sold 25.62, bought back 47.03).

THAT LOSS IS SUNK. Whether to hold or close the CURRENT 1500/1540 depends on
what it is worth now against what exiting costs. A past loss does not change
that, and folding it into the basis would make the ceiling and the stall fire
on history rather than on the position -- a ceiling computed against 42.17
would sit above the structure's own 40-dollar width and could never fire at
all.

WHERE IT DOES MISLEAD IS TOTAL P&L, and that is worth stating because the
per-structure numbers look better than the campaign:

    2 x 1500 long                          -12,224
    1530  sold +2,562, bought back -4,703   -2,141   <- the roll
    1550  sold                              +2,700
    1540  sold                              +4,008
                              net invested  -7,657
    at expiry above 1550: 4,000 + 5,000     +9,000
                                     P&L    +1,343

The two structures imply +3,540 between them. The campaign is +1,343. The
difference is the roll, and no per-structure view will ever show it -- a
structure knows what it cost, not what was spent getting to it.

THE GENERAL SHAPE, worth remembering: EXIT RULES SHOULD READ THE STRUCTURE,
REPORTING SHOULD READ THE CAMPAIGN. Mixing them makes exits act on sunk costs
or makes P&L flatter itself, and both were one small decision away here.

70. THE CREDIT SIDE WAS NEVER EXERCISED, AND CARRIED TWO INVERSIONS  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The intrinsic work in section 67 was written and tested against a book of
DEBIT spreads. Both places it touched had the sign backwards for credit
structures, and neither was reachable until a credit spread appeared.

ONE. _decompose SUBTRACTED FROM THE WIDTH.

    if st["credit"]: intrinsic = width - intrinsic

open_structures assigns the SHORT leg to the low strike on a call credit and
the high strike on a put credit, so `lo`/`hi` already resolve to the short
strike and the base formula already yields the cost to buy the structure back.
The subtraction turned maximum profit into maximum loss.

Live, QQQ 719/722 call credit, ten lots, QQQ at 718.74 -- BELOW the short, so
both legs expire worthless and the cost to close is ZERO. It reported 3.00.

TWO. THE STOP GUARD USED ONE COMPARISON FOR BOTH.

    intrinsic_ok = parts_iv[0] > abs(st["entry"])

Right for a debit: intrinsic is the VALUE at expiry, above what was paid means
profitable, so a stop reading a depressed mark should stand down. Backwards for
a credit: intrinsic is the COST TO CLOSE, so above the credit collected means a
LOSS -- and the guard suppressed the stop on exactly the credit spreads that
most needed it.

TOGETHER, on that live 0DTE position, the stall read roughly -711% instead of
+100% and the stop could not fire at all. Both rules pointed the wrong way at
once, on ten contracts, and the log looked entirely ordinary.

WHAT MADE THEM INVISIBLE FOR A DAY: every position in the book was a debit
spread. The engine's own AFTERNOON_CREDIT window sells credit spreads, so this
would have reached the engine's own trades as soon as one opened.

FIXED AND VERIFIED ACROSS ALL FOUR STRUCTURE TYPES -- call and put, debit and
credit -- at maximum profit, maximum loss, and between:

    call debit  above/below/between   10.00 / 0.00 / 5.00
    put  debit  below/above/between   10.00 / 0.00 / 5.00
    call credit above/below/between   10.00 / 0.00 / 5.00   (cost to close)
    put  credit below/above/between   10.00 / 0.00 / 5.00   (cost to close)

THE LESSON IS ABOUT COVERAGE, NOT ARITHMETIC. Section 67's reasoning was sound
and its implementation was tested against the only structures then open. A
sign convention that is correct for one structure and inverted for another
will not announce itself: it produces plausible numbers, in a log line that
reads normally, until the untested case appears. Any rule touching value,
intrinsic or return now needs checking against all four before it is trusted --
which is why the twelve-case check above is recorded rather than described.

71. THE STALL DECIDED ON INTRINSIC AND SOLD AT THE MARK  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section 67 moved the stall from the mark to intrinsic, which was right and
incomplete. It fixed WHEN the rule fires. It did not fix WHAT PRICE the rule
sells at, and those are different problems.

The stall decides on intrinsic and executes at the MARK. On a deep in-the-money
spread the mark sits below the entry, so a small intrinsic decline -- genuine,
correctly detected, exactly what the rule is for -- triggers a sale at a price
that books a LOSS on a position that was profitable at expiry.

Three closes, about 263 dollars, each firing legitimately by its own logic:

    AVGO 365/355  -113   intrinsic  9.40 vs 7.88 entry   (+193 at expiry)
    NVDA 220/230   -15   intrinsic  9.54 vs 8.15 entry   (+139 at expiry)
    MU   930/950  -135   intrinsic 20.00 vs 12.90 entry  (+710 at expiry)

And one that worked: SNDK 1500/1560 +824, where the mark was above entry and
the sale realised a real gain.

THE RULE NOW REQUIRES THAT THE EXIT ACTUALLY BOOK A GAIN. If the mark is below
entry there is no profit to protect and the case belongs to the STOP, which is
already intrinsic-aware. The two then divide cleanly: THE STALL PROTECTS GAINS,
THE STOP LIMITS LOSSES, and neither does the other's job badly.

PANW was next. At -115 on the mark and +415 at expiry, it would have been the
fourth. It is blocked now.

THE PATTERN ACROSS 67, 70 AND 71 IS ONE MISTAKE MADE THREE WAYS. Every exit
rule in this engine was written as "a return", and a return needs three things
named that were never named: WHICH VALUE (mark, intrinsic, value-at-expiry),
WHICH DIRECTION (a debit's intrinsic rises with profit, a credit's falls), and
WHICH PRICE THE EXIT ACTUALLY GETS. Getting one right and the others wrong
produces plausible numbers and normal-looking logs, which is why all three
survived until a position was live enough to expose them.

STILL OPEN: SNDK 1500/1560 is skipped -- "no fill price" -- after a roll and a
close left no recoverable entry for one leg. It is unmanaged rather than
mismanaged, which is the correct failure, but it is unmanaged.

72. ROLLING THE SAME STRIKES TWICE HID A POSITION FROM THE LADDER  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A SNDK 1500/1560 sat unmanaged for an hour behind two defects, both triggered
by rolling to the same strikes twice in one session.

THE CACHE WAS A MIRROR WHEN IT SHOULD HAVE BEEN A RECORD.

    state["structures"] = { st["key"]: {...} for st in structures }

Assigned, not merged -- so any entry the current pass could not rebuild was
erased within a minute. That included hand-seeded ones, which is how the
problem was first noticed: the seed vanished between cycles. Entries should be
dropped when a structure CLOSES, at the close site, not because one pass failed
to price it.

Why the pass could not price it: the 1500 was bought the PREVIOUS session, and
/orders is session-only (section 60), so today's fills net its quantity to zero
and filled_legs reports nothing.

AND A DEAD PAIR BLOCKED THE CACHED ONE. An opened-and-closed 1500/1560 leaves a
qty 0 entry in the order-derived book. The cache merge skipped any key already
present, so the corpse blocked the cached copy of the REOPENED structure at the
same strikes -- then dropped out itself as qty 0, leaving both legs to fall
through to inferred pairing, where they were skipped for want of a price.

Neither defect is reachable without reusing strikes. Roll a position back to a
level it previously occupied and the pairing sees a live pair, a dead pair and
two loose legs describing the same two contracts.

THE FAILURE MODE WAS CORRECT EVEN WHILE THE OUTCOME WAS WRONG. The position was
UNMANAGED, not MISMANAGED: the code refused to compute a return without a true
entry rather than guessing one. That is the behaviour to keep -- section 64's
lesson was that a wrong number in a healthy-looking log costs more than a
missing one -- but it means "no line in the log" has to be read as loudly as a
bad line.

73. THE QUOTE GUARD REFUSED TO ACT ON EXACTLY THE POSITIONS IT MATTERED FOR
    (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

_quotes_tradeable exists so the orphan manager does not close a position on a
garbage quote -- a leg quoted 1.00/2.50 is not a market, and acting on its mid
books an imaginary price. It rejected a leg whose bid-ask spread exceeded 25%
of the mid, or whose bid was zero, and it required BOTH legs to pass.

Three things were wrong with that, and they compound at the end of a 0DTE
session.

ONE. THE FAR LEG IS SUPPOSED TO GO TO ZERO.

On a QQQ 719/722 call credit at 15:49, the 722 was quoted 0.00 / 0.01. That is
not a broken quote, it is the correct quote: the option is worthless and
nobody will bid for it. The guard read the zero bid as an unusable market, so
the FORCE_CLOSE decided at 15:45 logged

    QQQ 719/722  -- FORCE_CLOSE [observation only]

every cycle from 15:49 to the bell and never fired.

TWO. A PENNY-WIDE MARKET FAILS A PERCENTAGE TEST.

Judging the short leg alone is not enough. That 719 was quoted 0.02 / 0.03 --
one cent wide, as tight as an option can be quoted -- which is 40% of its own
2.5-cent mid. A proportional threshold rejects every cheap option, and by the
close everything in a 0DTE book is cheap. 25% of mid is a real test on a $6
leg and noise on a $0.03 one.

THREE. IT HELD ONE STANDARD FOR EVERY DECISION.

A width test protects a judgment about VALUE. Ceiling, stall and stop all read
the mark, and a false mark makes all three fire wrongly. FORCE_CLOSE is not a
judgment about value: it exists because these spreads are physically settled
and an in-the-money short gets ASSIGNED. Against assignment, a poor fill is a
good outcome. Holding the force close to a standard designed to protect the
accuracy of a profit calculation gets the trade-off backwards.

WHAT IT NOW DOES:

  - judges the SHORT leg only, the one that must be bought back and the one
    carrying the risk. A long bid at zero is INFORMATION -- it is worthless --
    not an unusable quote, and the mark already values it at zero, correctly.
  - tests the ASK, not the bid. Closing BUYS the short back, so a zero bid on
    a nearly-worthless short stops nothing; a zero ask means no offer to buy
    at, and that does.
  - passes a quote tight in CENTS or tight in PROPORTION, whichever is kinder:
    TRADING_ORPHAN_MAX_LEG_SPREAD_ABS (0.05) alongside the existing 25%.
  - drops the width test for FORCE_CLOSE, which now needs only a real market
    to trade into rather than a tight one.

Verified across the seven quote shapes that occur:

    quote                                      value rules   FORCE_CLOSE
    0DTE far leg 0.00/0.01, short 0.02/0.03    ACT           ACT
    short 3.50/3.60, long quoted wide          ACT           ACT
    short 1.00/2.50, no real market            refuse        ACT
    short 0.00/0.05, no bid but offered        ACT           ACT
    short not quoted at all                    refuse        refuse
    short 0.00/0.00, no market either side     refuse        refuse
    short crossed 2.00/1.00                    refuse        refuse

THAT DAY IT WAS FREE, AND THAT IS THE POINT. QQQ closed 717.47, 1.53 below the
short; both legs expired worthless and holding paid the full +370, more than
closing would have. The bug cost nothing and would never have appeared in P&L.
It surfaced only because the log line was read.

THE RISK IT LEFT IS NOT SYMMETRIC. A blocked force close is harmless when the
short finishes out of the money, and is an ASSIGNMENT when it does not -- and
the short is only in the money on the days the force close exists for. The
guard failed safe on the days it was not needed and would have failed open on
the days it was. Three positions carry into 2026-09-04, each reaching its own
final hour.

THE GENERAL FORM, the same shape as section 70: a threshold calibrated in one
regime, applied in another where its units stop meaning what they meant. 25%
of mid is a real test at $6 and noise at $0.03, the way a sign convention
correct for a debit is inverted for a credit. Neither announces itself. Both
produce a plausible number in a log line that reads normally.

AND A SECOND, NARROWER RULE: A GUARD SHOULD KNOW WHAT IT IS GUARDING. One
tradeability test served four decisions with different stakes -- three about
how much money to book, one about whether shares get delivered. Asking what
the refusal COSTS in each case is what separated them.

74. THE CREDIT STALL AND THE MORNING RIDE SHARED ONE TIMER  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STALL_MINUTES and STALL_GIVEBACK_PCT drove two different rules:

    nodes.py:1958   stalled        = quiet >= STALL_MINUTES and ...   the ride
    nodes.py:2090   credit_stalled = quiet >= STALL_MINUTES and ...   the credit

So the two could not be tuned apart -- not in a sweep, and not in production
either, where they are the same two environment variables.

THIS IS SECTION 55 ONE LAYER DOWN. That section records sweep_creditstall
attributing +7.47/day to the credit stall when its arms were also retiming the
morning ride, and a deployment decision being made on the number. The harness
shadowing that caused it was fixed. The SHARED KNOB was not, so the arms still
moved both rules -- the report filters to credit trades, which handles the
direct contamination, but the ride's changes still move the account's
compounding equity within an arm, and that feeds credit position sizing.

A weaker version of the same defect, and the same shape: the number was real,
the attribution was not.

THE TWO RULES WANT DIFFERENT TIMERS ANYWAY, which is the part that makes this
more than tidiness. On the morning ride the profit curve tracks spot, so a few
quiet minutes genuinely means the move is done. On a 0DTE credit spread with
spot sitting still, THETA prints a new high nearly every cycle -- so an absence
of new highs is not quiet, it means the underlying has come back toward the
short strike and decay has stopped winning. That is a different signal arriving
on a different clock. One number serving both makes at most one of them right.

NOW SPLIT: TRADING_CREDIT_STALL_MINUTES and TRADING_CREDIT_STALL_GIVEBACK_PCT.
Unset, each falls back to the ride's value, so nothing deployed changes:

    deployed today (credit unset)   ride 5.0/3.3   credit 5.0/3.3
    credit timer only               ride 5.0/3.3   credit 12.0/3.3
    both split                      ride 5.0/3.3   credit 12.0/8.0
    empty string is unset           ride 5.0/3.3   credit 5.0/3.3

THE SPLIT EXISTS TO MAKE THE QUESTION ASKABLE, NOT TO ANSWER IT. No value
changes on the droplet as a result of this.

AND THE SWEEP HAD TO CHANGE WITH IT. sweep_creditstall now holds the ride at
the deployed timer in every arm and moves only the credit knobs.

ONE TRAP FOUND WHILE WRITING IT, worth recording because it would have
reproduced the original bug exactly. The late-stall test retimes the stall from
inside replay_session, which is shared by EVERY sweep. Written
unconditionally, that assignment overwrote whatever knobs the current sweep had
just set -- so sweep_creditstall would have run eight arms with identical
settings and reported eight plausible, slightly different rows. It is now
gated on the late-stall test actually being the one running, and the globals it
writes are restored when it finishes.

THE PATTERN TO WATCH: shared mutable module state, written per-bar by one

77. HOLDING THROUGH THE OPEN: LOSSES WAIT, PROFITS DO NOT  (2026-09-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three positions carry into Friday 2026-09-04 -- PANW 310/327.5 C, DELL 530/520
P, SNDK 1500/1560 C -- all expiring that day, all deep in the money, all
managed by orphans.py.

THE OPEN IS NOT A SIGNAL, IT IS A SPREAD. In the first stretch of the session
the book is at its widest and the mark at its least trustworthy. A structure
can print a "peak" on one wide quote and a giveback on the next, or show -25%
on a spread that has not moved, with nothing having happened to the
underlying. On a Friday expiry that dips early and comes back, both the stop
and the stall fire on microstructure.

TRADING_ORPHAN_HOLD_UNTIL gates them. Empty by default; set to 10:00 for
Friday.

THE ASYMMETRY IS THE WHOLE POINT, and I had it backwards on the first pass.
I gated the stall and left the stop live, reasoning that a stop exists FOR a
bad open. The correction: taking MAX PROFIT at 09:30 is fine, taking a LOSS at
09:30 is not. Those are not symmetric decisions and should not share a gate.

  GATED
    the stop     a -25% mark at 09:31 is as likely to be a wide quote as a
                 real move. Booking a loss on it is unrecoverable; waiting
                 thirty minutes to confirm it costs nothing if it is real.
    the stall    books a WINNER early on a judgment about a mark, which is
                 the judgment the open is worst at making.

  LIVE FROM THE FIRST CYCLE
    the ceiling  90% of maximum. A decision about a position that has already
                 won, and the number is not in doubt at any hour. Taking it at
                 09:35 is a good outcome.
    force close  assignment, not value. 15:45 regardless.

Verified at 09:31, 09:59, 10:00 and 15:50 with the knob unset, set to 10:00,
and set to an unparseable value.

WHAT IT COSTS, STATED PLAINLY. It trades tail protection for noise immunity,
knowingly. These are DEBIT spreads, so loss is bounded by the premium paid
rather than by the stop:

                        debit paid     capped by a -25% stop
    PANW  310/327.5 C      1,335                    334
    DELL  530/520   P        628                    157
    SNDK 1500/1560  C      3,184                    796
                           -----                  -----
                           5,147                  1,287

So the gate puts up to about 3,860 dollars of additional exposure at risk in a
genuine gap-down between 09:30 and 10:00, in exchange for not booking losses
into an opening spread that reverses. On 0DTE a 2% adverse move frequently
does not come back. That is the trade, made deliberately, and it is the
accepted decision rather than the maximum-expectancy one.

WHERE THE FLOOR IS after 10:00. STOP_RESPECTS_INTRINSIC means the stop stays
suppressed while a position still pays at expiry, and re-arms below:

    PANW  entry 13.35   stop re-arms below   323.35
    DELL  entry  6.28   stop re-arms above   523.72
    SNDK  entry 31.84   stop re-arms below 1,531.84

FAILS OPEN ON A BAD VALUE. An unparseable time leaves both rules working
normally rather than silently disabling two exits for a whole session -- the
opposite of the quote guard in section 73, which failed closed and blocked an
exit nobody noticed for eleven minutes.

TRADING_ORPHAN_INTRINSIC_GIVEBACK is a stop measured on the value AT EXPIRY
rather than on the mark: it fires when intrinsic falls N dollars from its own
peak on a structure whose peak was above entry. It deliberately does NOT
require booking a gain -- the whole case for it is that the mark is under
water and holding is getting worse.

OFF BY DEFAULT (0), and left off. It is a real exit rule, not a correctness

    SNDK 1700/1720 scaled up, average entry 11.60 -> 13.20

79. THE EXIT LADDER NEVER ASKED WHETHER AN ORDER WAS ALREADY WORKING
    (2026-09-04)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

orphans.review() submitted a close whenever a rule fired, with no check for an
order already resting on the same legs. A manual limit left working and the
15:45 flatten could BOTH fill -- selling the structure twice and leaving a
short leg naked into expiry, on the one day of its life when that is
unrecoverable.

FOUND BY CAUSING IT. A limit was placed by hand at 35.00 on a managed SNDK
1730/1690, and only then did the question get asked. It was worked around that
afternoon with an external watcher that cancelled the resting order five
minutes before the flatten. That worked -- the order filled at 21.84 before it
mattered -- but "remember to run a watcher whenever you place a manual limit"
is not a mechanism, it is a thing waiting to be forgotten.

THE BROKER IS THE AUTHORITY, not local state. working_leg_symbols() asks
Tradier which option symbols sit under a non-terminal order. Orders placed
from a phone, by hand, or by a previous run of this process all count, and
none of them would appear in anything the engine tracked itself. One call per
review pass, not one per structure.

When a rule fires on a structure with a working order, the engine logs what it
wanted and stands down:

    ORPHAN SNDK 1730/1690 wants FORCE_CLOSE but an order is already working
    on its legs — standing down rather than selling it twice.

FAILS OPEN, DELIBERATELY. If the orders endpoint errors the guard returns an
empty set and the ladder behaves exactly as it did before. An unreadable
orders list should not silently disable every exit rule -- that trade is the
wrong way round, and section 73 is the record of what a guard that fails
closed costs.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO: cancel the resting order. Standing down means the manual
order owns the position, which is right -- a human put it there deliberately
-- but it also means a limit resting too far from the market can hold the
flatten off until the bell. The watcher pattern is still the answer for that,
and it now protects against a missed exit rather than a double one.

80. THE GIVEBACK RULE SOLD INTO WOBBLES, AND COST 2,412 IN ONE AFTERNOON
    (2026-09-04)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section 78 added TRADING_ORPHAN_INTRINSIC_GIVEBACK_PCT to cover the band where
the stall and the stop are both correctly silent. It was written OFF and the
notes said it needed a sweep before anyone turned it on.

It was turned on the same afternoon, at 15% of width, on a live book. Within
fifteen minutes it closed three positions:

                    booked    holding would have been    cost
    SNDK 1700/1720    -750                       -513    -237
    MU   990/1000     -390                     +1,660  -2,050
    QQQ  715/719      -375                       -250    -125
                                                  NET  -2,412

WHY IT FAILED, and it is not bad luck. MU 990/1000's intrinsic through the
twenty minutes before it fired:

    17:26  10.00   17:33  10.00   17:39  10.00
    17:29   9.22   17:34   9.65   17:40  10.00
    17:30   9.10   17:35   9.96   17:41   9.54
    17:31   9.62   17:36  10.00   17:42   8.55
    17:32   9.70   17:37  10.00   17:43   7.89  <- fired

THE POSITION BREATHED 8.68 TO 10.00 ALL AFTERNOON. Routine swings of 1.32
against a threshold of 1.50. The rule was sitting a fifth of a point above the
noise floor, so the first slightly larger wobble tripped it. The dip lasted
THREE MINUTES and MU was back at full intrinsic within the hour.

TWO THINGS WERE WRONG, and only one of them is the threshold.

The threshold was too tight, but no threshold fixes this on its own: the size
of a wobble is a property of the UNDERLYING, not of the structure, and a
number chosen per-structure cannot know it. Widening it enough to clear MU's
noise would have made it useless on a quiet name.

TIME distinguishes them. A reversal is still there five minutes later; a
wobble is not. TRADING_ORPHAN_GIVEBACK_CONFIRM_MIN (default 5) requires the
giveback to be CONTINUOUSLY true for that long, and any new intrinsic high
clears the clock, so a dip-recover-dip pattern starts over rather than
accumulating.

Replayed against MU's real sequence: the old rule fires at 17:43, the new one
never fires -- the dip held for one minute of the five required.

This is the same conclusion section 43 reached for the morning ride, by the
same route, and the stall has had a quiet timer ever since. The giveback rule
was written without one because it was conceived as a STOP -- and a stop does
fire immediately. But this is not a stop. A stop reads a level; this reads a
CHANGE, and a change needs a duration to be real.

THE PROCESS FAILURE IS THE POINT. The rule was measured against one scenario
-- MU draining at 13:00 -- and shipped on it. That scenario was real and the
rule addressed it. What was never asked is what the rule does on every OTHER
shape the same book produces, and the answer was available: the engine had
been logging intrinsic every minute for all of them. Nobody looked until it
had cost 2,412 dollars.

STILL UNSWEPT. The confirmation timer makes it defensible, not measured. Both
knobs go through the harness before this is trusted, and the default of 5
minutes is borrowed from the stall rather than derived.
