================================================================================
QQQ 0DTE TRADING ENGINE - STRATEGY NOTES
================================================================================

Current as of 2026-08-22. Every number here is read from the deployed
configuration, not from intent.

This file describes WHAT the engine does. The WHY lives beside each parameter
in trading_engine/playbook.py and trading_engine/nodes.py, where the sweep
that chose it is quoted in full. Nothing here is a preference: every value was
measured with scripts/sweep.py over 60 sessions, and several were measured
more than once because the tool producing the evidence turned out to be wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE ENGINE TRADES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

QQQ options expiring the same day. Vertical spreads only - never a naked leg,
never a single long option. One position at a time, held in a single
trading_open_positions row.

Two structures:

  DEBIT VERTICAL (morning). Buy the nearer strike, sell the further one. Pays
  if QQQ moves the right way; loses the premium paid if it does not.

  CREDIT VERTICAL (afternoon). Sell the nearer strike, buy the further one for
  protection. Pays if the short strike holds, whichever way price wanders
  below it. Risks width-minus-credit.

Two windows are live. Two more are defined and switched off - they stay in the
code so a position opened under one can still be priced and exited if it is
ever re-enabled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TRADING DAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  09:30  Market opens. No entries for 15 minutes - the opening auction
         produces whipsaws the indicators read as trend.
  09:45  Warmup ends. Short entries become possible (long entries are not
         restricted by this).
  10:15  MORNING_DRIFT opens.
  11:30  MORNING_DRIFT closes to new entries. A position already open keeps
         running.
  13:25  Handoff. Any morning position is closed so the slot is free.
  13:30  AFTERNOON_CREDIT opens.
  14:00  Debit cutoff - no new DEBIT entries after this hour. Credit windows
         are exempt: a credit spread wants less time left, not more.
  15:00  AFTERNOON_CREDIT closes to new entries.
  15:45  Force close. Everything is flattened regardless of P&L. Expiry is
         16:15; this leaves half an hour of contract life so the exit is
         priced with real time value rather than marked to intrinsic.

A cron job runs one cycle a minute (scripts/run_cycle.py). It owns the
market-hours check itself, in America/New_York, because a UTC crontab window
would be wrong for half the year.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. INDICATORS - EXACTLY WHAT IS COMPUTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All are computed over 5-minute QQQ bars, with five days of history so the
longer averages are warmed up. (A backtest that used only the current
session's bars produced materially different results - see section 11.)

  MACD          12/26/9 EMAs. Histogram > 0.01 is BULLISH, < -0.01 BEARISH,
                otherwise NEUTRAL.

  Trend         EMA(20) and EMA(50), not simple averages. ABOVE_SMA requires
  (sma_trend)   the close above BOTH. EMA rather than SMA so this filter reacts
                on the same timescale as MACD - a laggy SMA confirms a trend
                after most of a 0DTE day's move is gone.

  9 EMA         Trailing reference only, never an entry filter. ABOVE_EMA9 /
                BELOW_EMA9.

  EMA cross     EMA(9) against EMA(20) - velocity rather than position.

  50 EMA        The bar's HIGH pierces the 50 EMA but the CLOSE finishes below
  rejection     it. Recorded; used only by the REJECT tier.

  VWAP          Session VWAP. Falls back to an unweighted typical price when
                the bar volume disagrees with Tradier's session volume, which
                it frequently does.

  RSI           14-period, Wilder smoothing. OVERBOUGHT >= 70, OVERSOLD <= 30.
                Separately, a BAND reading: BULL_BAND is RSI 50-65 and rising,
                BEAR_BAND is 35-48 and falling. The band, not the extreme, is
                what the CLEAN tier uses.

  Bollinger     20-period, 2 standard deviations. Zone is UPPER_BAND /
                LOWER_BAND / NORMAL; a midline cross is recorded as CROSS_UP /
                CROSS_DOWN. The 20-period standard deviation is published as
                bollinger_sd and is the fallback for credit strike placement.

  ADX           Wilder ADX(14). TRENDING >= 22, otherwise CHOPPY. Recorded on
                every cycle and gates nothing - it was tested as a gate and did
                not earn one.

  Session move  Close against the regular-hours open, in percent. Published so
                a session-level trend filter could be tested; that filter is
                off (section 10).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW AN ENTRY IS DECIDED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An entry needs, in order: no kill switch, no daily-loss halt, no loss-streak
halt, a window open, a tier that fires, that window accepting that tier, the
direction allowed, the cooldown clear, and size greater than zero.

The tier ladder, most selective first. Only the first match applies.

  CLEAN     (enabled)  Four conditions agreeing. Bull: above the 20/50 EMAs,
                       EMA9 above EMA20, above VWAP, RSI in the bull band, and
                       macro GOOD. Bear: the mirror, without the macro
                       requirement.
  STRICT    (enabled)  MACD, trend, a Bollinger pierce AND an RSI extreme.
  RELAXED   (enabled)  STRICT without the RSI requirement.
  REJECT    (enabled)  A 50 EMA rejection with trend and VWAP already down.
                       Bearish only.
  MOMENTUM  (off)
  TREND     (off)

MORNING_DRIFT accepts CLEAN only, and long only. Its bear side was measured at
27% wins and -50.62 a trade, negative in both sample halves.

AFTERNOON_CREDIT accepts any tier, either direction: a bullish read sells puts
below spot, a bearish read sells calls above.

Entries are refused for 30 minutes on the side that just lost, and the whole
session stands down after 3 consecutive losses (AFTERNOON_CREDIT is exempt
from the streak halt; the dollar caps still bind it).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE MACRO GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Once a cycle, market_signals_agent builds a verdict from four inputs and asks
Claude for GOOD or BAD, plus a confidence and a written reason. Both are
logged so a flip can be explained afterwards rather than guessed at.

  - Advance/decline breadth across the Nasdaq-100, self-computed from Tradier
    quotes. Index breadth rather than full-market breadth, because QQQ tracks
    the index.
  - The VIX. At or above 22.0 the engine halts new entries in both directions -
    disorder is not directional.
  - Freshly scraped headlines.
  - The three nearest past headlines from pgvector (market_news_vectors,
    Voyage-4 embeddings, cosine distance).

market_sentiment GOOD is required for BULLISH entries only. A bearish setup
does not need it: the conditions that make being long uncomfortable are the
conditions a short position profits from.

Crude oil and the 10-year yield are fetched and logged every cycle and gate
nothing. Measured against the engine's own daily P&L, neither carries a usable
signal at decision time - both extremes beat the middle, in opposite
directions, which is the shape of noise. See section 10.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. HOW A POSITION EXITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Checked in this order, every cycle:

  FORCE CLOSE   15:45, regardless of P&L.

  If the window RIDES (MORNING_DRIFT):
    Ride ceiling  90% of the structure's maximum profit. A spread cannot be
                  worth more than its width, and inside the last tenth there is
                  no move left to ride - only convergence, held through the
                  widest quotes of the day. Expressed as a share of max profit
                  rather than a return, because the two differ per entry.
    Stop loss     -20% of premium paid.
    Handoff       13:25. The slot goes to the credit window.
    Otherwise     Hold. There is no take-profit and no ratchet on a ride;
                  both were tested and both cost money (section 10).

  Otherwise (AFTERNOON_CREDIT):
    Target        Above 50% of the credit collected the trail ARMS rather than
                  selling. Above 90% it books outright - the last tenth is the
                  worst-paid, since buying a spread back at 0.04 costs the same
                  two-legged spread as buying it at 0.20.
    Trail break   The 9 EMA turning against the structure, but ONLY when spot
                  is within $2 of the short strike. Direction alone does not
                  threaten short premium; distance to the strike does.
    Ratchet       Hands back more than 30% of its peak (minimum 10 points).
                  This is the exit that fires most often.
    Stop loss     -100% of the credit - the classic buy-it-back-at-twice rule.
    Risk-off      A tighter -13% stop for LONG positions while macro is BAD.
                  Never applied to shorts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. PRICING - WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Entries and marks come from Tradier's live option chain, with greeks. The
normal-CDF model in broker.py is the fallback when the chain is missing a
strike.

  Entry marks at the NATURAL price: a vertical sold fills at its bid, one
  bought fills at its ask.
  An open position marks at the MID - what it is worth, as opposed to what a
  fill costs.

This replaced model pricing on 2026-08-21 after one session of logging both
side by side. The model priced an open credit spread at 0.20-0.34 all
afternoon while the market quoted 0.01-0.04, an 88-97% overstatement of what
closing it cost, and the exit rules read that number.

Every cycle still logs both, as a "Chain vs model" line, so the gap stays
visible.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SIZING AND RISK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Position size is the smallest of four independent limits.

  1. Entry fraction     15% of equity, per trade (MORNING_DRIFT overrides to
                        8%). This is capital deployed.
  2. Risk share         A window's share of the day's loss budget. Both live
                        windows: 50%. Sized so ONE stop-out cannot exceed it.
                        Post-loss re-entries use 30% and are halved.
  3. Tail cap           15% of equity at STRUCTURAL risk - premium paid for a
                        debit, width-minus-credit for a credit. This is the
                        only limit that governs what the market can impose
                        rather than what the rules intend, and it is usually
                        what binds.
  4. Minimum credit     A credit spread priced below $0.05 is refused outright.

Session-level circuit breakers:

  Daily loss cap        6% of the equity the session started with. Past it, no
                        new entries; open positions are still managed, because
                        refusing to manage what you hold is not risk control.
  Loss streak           3 consecutive losses stands the session down.
  Kill switch           KILL_SWITCH.txt halts everything.

Equity is realised: starting budget plus every closed trade. Size follows the
account down after losses and up after wins.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. THE TWO LIVE WINDOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MORNING_DRIFT - 10:15 to 11:30, rides to 13:25
  Structure     $6 wide, long leg $2 in the money, short leg $4 out. Bullish
                only, CLEAN tier only.
  Sizing        8% entry fraction, 50% risk share.
  Exits         -20% stop, 90%-of-max ceiling, 13:25 handoff. No take-profit.
  Evidence      Judged per day with the credit window live: +49.46/day against
                +41.42 for the $5 deep placement it replaced, both sample
                halves higher. It wins 42% of the time against 52% for the
                deep version - the average is better because the structure
                stays uncapped $4 higher, so trending sessions pay for the ones
                that stop out. 24 trades: provisional.

AFTERNOON_CREDIT - 13:30 to 15:00
  Structure     $4 wide. Short strike chosen by DELTA, targeting 0.20, from the
                live chain; volatility placement (3 standard deviations) is the
                fallback. Either direction.
  Sizing        15% entry fraction (global), 50% risk share.
  Exits         Arm at 50% of credit, book at 90%, 30% ratchet, -100% stop.
  Evidence      $4 is the only width whose sample halves are both positive:
                $1 -83.85/trade, $2 -13.17, $3 +26.88, $4 +32.50, $5 +17.16,
                $6 +11.25. Delta placement replaced sigma placement after the
                latter chose a 7-delta strike the market valued at $0.02.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. TESTED AND REJECTED - DO NOT RE-PROPOSE WITHOUT NEW EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each of these was built, measured over 60 sessions, and left switched off. The
knob usually still exists.

  Put debit spreads on down days      27% wins, -50.62/trade, negative in both
                                      halves. Down moves reverse faster than
                                      they continue.
  Morning iron condors                Short strikes touched on 74-80% of
                                      sessions at $4 out. A volatility squeeze
                                      does not identify a quiet morning: the
                                      calmest third holds 26% against 18%.
  More trades per day                 2.3 trades/day earns half of what 1.3
                                      earns, and multiplies the worst day by
                                      eleven.
  A longer morning window             Entries to 13:25 take +21.58/trade down
                                      to +1.74.
  Trend-only credit entries           Enters 17 minutes earlier and collects
                                      more decay; 89% wins becomes 76%.
  A ratchet on the morning ride       Worse at every arm level from +12% to
                                      +32%.
  A fixed-dollar ratchet offset       The $0.25 equivalent is the worst variant
                                      tested. A share of peak widens as the
                                      position wins; a fixed offset does not.
  Refusing longs on a down session    Costs 7-9 dollars a day and does not
                                      improve the worst day by a cent.
  Uncapping on strong ADX             Worse at every threshold tested.
  A bear call spread on bad mornings  38% wins, -57.64 a trade, negative in
                                      both halves. Same cause as the put debit
                                      spread: morning declines reverse, which
                                      bleeds a structure needing continuation
                                      and runs over one needing the strike to
                                      hold. The engine waits and sells calls at
                                      13:30 instead, where the strike survives
                                      92% of sessions against 48% at 10:15.
  A tighter credit stop near the bell  Worse at every setting, and the worst
                                      day did not improve by a cent.
  Yields and crude as gates           No usable signal at decision time.

Still open, with forward evidence accumulating: an iron condor at 13:30. It
beats either single side in every model-priced cell, but a condor sells four
far-OTM legs where a single spread sells two, so the model's known
overstatement flatters it roughly twice as much, and the four-leg bid-ask is
$9-11 against $2-15. The shadow condor logs market-priced marks at 09:45,
10:15 and 13:30, at two wing distances each, precisely to settle this.

Also open: VIX direction into 13:30 is the one macro input with a monotonic
relationship to the engine's P&L - falling +110.78/day at 68% green days,
rising -8.51 at 40%. Untested as a gate.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. HOW TO CHANGE SOMETHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Run the sweep. scripts/sweep.py replays past sessions through the engine's own
agents and its own execution_risk_agent, with the clock, the data feed and the
database swapped out, so a result is what the deployed code would have done.

  python scripts/sweep.py windows      every window traded solo
  python scripts/sweep.py daily        trades per day against dollars per day
  python scripts/sweep.py size         risk share and entry fraction
  python scripts/sweep.py placement    morning width and depth, per day
  python scripts/sweep.py credit       credit widths
  python scripts/sweep.py breach       model-free strike survival rates
  python scripts/sweep.py condorvs     both sides against one side
  python scripts/sweep.py macro        yields, crude and VIX against P&L
  python scripts/sweep.py daytype      P&L by market regime

Read the module docstring before trusting a number. What the sweep cannot see:
macro sentiment (assumed GOOD on every session), real fills, and any session
containing a gap through a short strike - the 60-day sample has none, which is
exactly the risk the tail cap exists to bound.

A parameter is worth changing when the winning cell wins in BOTH sample halves.
An average that reverses between halves is a result about the sample, not about
the parameter. Four of the last five ideas tested failed that bar.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. KNOWN LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - No orders are routed anywhere. The broker is a mock; fills are assumed.
    A resting stop at the exchange does not exist, so a gap through a short
    strike is bounded by the tail cap and by nothing else.
  - The credit window earns most of the P&L and cannot be validated by
    backtest: there are no historical option chains, and the model that would
    price them is the one being corrected. Its forward record, chain-priced
    since 2026-08-21, is the evidence that counts.
  - trade_setup_vectors stores every closed trade's setup and outcome and is
    not consulted by anything. It waits for enough trades that "three similar
    setups all lost" is signal rather than noise.
  - The morning window's entire record is 24 backtested trades.
